# Realm of Talos

2D roguelite party builder (Godot 4.7, GDScript). You command the last city and defend it from monsters for 42 days, six weeks. Currently: a playable run (a calendar of battle, free and boss days, with events at the start of some free days) with countdown battles, weapon proficiency, combat arts and magic that carry through the run.

## Run

Open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or from a shell:

```
godot --path .
```

### A run

- The game opens on a title screen (begin a new run, window or full screen, leave). A run opens with a letter to the commander naming the two companions who answered the call.
- The city is the hub, and it is a place: its buildings are the buttons. The **Gate** is where the day's business is (march out on battle and boss days, hear the matter that opens some free days, send a party beyond the walls or end a free day); the **Tavern** hires, the **Smithy** sells weapons, the **Inn** rests the company, the **Mason's Yard** mends the walls, trades one material for the other and builds up the city (always open, to look), the **Barracks** holds the party (always open), and the **Watchtower** reports the days ahead (always open). Buildings that cannot be used today are shuttered and say why; once the day's one action is spent the Inn, the Mason's Yard and the Drill Yard shut, while the Tavern, the Smithy and the Infirmary stay open, since they take only coin. The building to go to next has a glowing name board.
- The sky is the clock. A free day runs from morning to dusk once its action is spent; a battle day opens at dawn with the enemy's fires beyond the wall; a boss day has a red sky. A curtain with the day's number falls between days.
- A thin strip at the top shows the day, the next 7 days as medallions (swords = battle, sun = free day, letter = a free day that begins with an event, horned skull = boss; hover for details), the walls (city health, 100; at 0 the run is lost), gold, iron ore and timber. The company stands along the bottom as round portraits with their health as an arc; click one to open the barracks on them. A slip of parchment at the bottom left gives the day's news.
- Rules are not written on the screens: they are in tooltips, on the letters that buildings and events show before you commit, and in this file. Keys still work but are not drawn on buttons; a button's tooltip names its key. In the city: P barracks, G gate, W watchtower, Esc puts a letter down.
- 42 days in six weeks of seven. Every week has the same shape: battle, free, battle, free, battle, free, and then a seventh day that is a free day in weeks 1, 3 and 5 and a boss in weeks 2, 4 and 6 (days 14, 28 and 42). So battles and free days alternate, day 1 is a battle, and the day before every boss is free; the only breaks are the two free days that end weeks 1, 3 and 5 and the battle that follows each boss. A run has 18 battles, 3 bosses and 21 free days (`RunCalendar`).
- Events: one free day in every week begins with an event (six a run), never the day before a boss. The days are drawn when the run begins and show on the strip. An event does not repeat while unseen ones remain.
- The week: the Tavern's offers, the Smithy's stock and the training ground's drills are rolled on the first day of each week (days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36) and stay the same all week. Someone hired or a weapon bought is gone until the next week. What the Smithy sells climbs with the weeks (see Weapons).
- You start with 2 random companions of the fourteen (ten fighters, two for each weapon, and four mages, two for each school; at least one of the two is never a mage). The two who share a weapon are built differently: Elena is all health and strength where Asterion is armour, Aina is balanced where Kaito is strength and crits with no defence, Erik is accurate where Koharu is swift and lucky, Ingrid is sturdy where Raizen is fast, Yuzu is speed and crits where Shiro is tough, Aveline heals where Elio protects, and Freya is a glass cannon where Sylas is slow, hardy and hinders. Everyone starts with 16-24 health (Elena the most, Elio the least) and 12-20 stamina (every fighter more than every mage), and with the wooden weapon of their type; the armory also starts with one wooden weapon of every type the two starters' weapons do not cover (so the company owns every wooden weapon; a hire later brings only their own). Up to 4 are fielded, left to right (rightmost = nearest the enemies); the rest wait in reserve. In the Barracks the company stands in its line behind the muster rail, rear to front, with the reserve behind the bench's board below (the art is cut out, so they stand free against the wall; each stands in a bay of their own between timber posts, and nobody's art covers a neighbour): carry someone to another place or to the bench to move them (someone from the bench set down on a full line changes places with whoever stood there), or use the orders on their page (keys: left and right arrows, F, A). The page on the right is the muster roll for whoever is selected: their stats, each with a line under it as long as its growth rate (the chance it rises by 1 on a level-up, with the figure beside it), what they hold and their Attack with it (attack power for that kind of weapon plus its Mt: what a strike deals before the target's defence; each weapon they could take shows the Attack it would give), and the armory to take a weapon from (a weapon not allowed says why: the grade it needs). It also opens the combat arts screen. All of this is free on any day until you commit to the day's activity.
- Levels: every companion starts at level 1. A won battle grants an XP pool (by danger, growing with the day; shown on each front) shared among the companions fielded in it, injured ones included; reserve companions and lost battles get none. A quarter of the pool is split evenly, so someone who did little still gains a little; the other three quarters are split by the contribution points each earned in that battle: a landed Attack 2 (a miss 1), a combat art used 3, a spell cast 3 (of any kind), each enemy blow that lands on them 1 (2 while guarding), and 4 more for landing the killing blow on an enemy (an art that kills several earns it for each; a kill by bleeding, poison or blight is whoever applied it, a kill by a reflecting stance or ward is whoever carries it). Focus and Wait earn nothing. If nobody earned a point the whole pool is split evenly. Shares are rounded down and the XP left over goes 1 at a time to the highest scorers, so the whole pool is always handed out (`Levels.split_xp`; the points are `BattleConfig.POINTS_*`, the even share `RunConfig.XP_EVEN_SHARE_PERCENT`). On each level-up every stat but BLD rolls its own growth rate (each companion's own, about 40% on average) to rise by 1, and at least 2 stats always rise. Max HP gained raises current HP too. Tavern hires join at the roster's average level, rounded down. A companion fielded in every battle in a party of four who earns an average share ends the run around level 13.
- HP carries over between days. At the end of every day each companion not in the Infirmary heals 20% of max HP (rounded up; some traits change it: see Traits). Stamina is full at the start of every battle. Nobody is ever removed from the company: a companion injured in battle goes to the Infirmary for 4 days (see Infirmary). Healing spells are cast only in battle: between battles the company recovers by the daily heal and by Rest at the Inn.
- **Battle day**: the Gate opens the war table with 2 or 3 fronts (keys 1-3), each with its enemies, a danger of 1-3, the reward and the city health lost if the battle is lost (15/20/25). Fronts you do not pick have no consequence. Enemies get stronger as the days pass.
- **Boss day**: one front. Losing ends the run; beating the day 42 boss wins it. Each boss day draws one of three bosses when the run begins (see Bosses). Until the Watchtower is built up the boss days ahead are not marked and their bosses not named (see The city's upgrades). The day strip marks the boss's day with a horned skull, and when the next boss day is beyond the days shown, one more medallion after a gap marks it; hover a boss medallion, or climb the Watchtower, for every boss still ahead with its one-line warning. The boss card shows the boss large, its name, what makes it dangerous and any escorts.
- **Free day**: 1 action, one of: rest at the Inn (everyone not in the Infirmary to full HP and every spell back to full uses; otherwise uses come back only on leaving the Infirmary and, one a night, with the Inn's second level), mend the walls at the Mason's Yard (3 timber for 20 city health; see The city's upgrades), train one companion at the training ground (see Training ground), send a party scavenging beyond the walls (see Scavenging). Besides it, and before or after it, any number of errands that cost gold but never the action: buy at the Smithy (weapons from the week's stock, one of each; see Weapons for what it sells), hire at the Tavern (from the week's 1-2 companions offered, mages included), cure companions in the Infirmary (see Infirmary), build up the city's facilities, swap materials at the Mason's Yard, enchant weapons at the Smithy (see The city's upgrades and Enchanting). Errands also wait for the day's event (`Run.errand_block_reason`). End the day at the Gate when done; an unused action is lost.
- **Event**: on a free day that begins with one, a letter with a short story and 2-3 choices (keys 1-3; it can be put down and picked up again at the Gate) that trade gold, materials, city health, companions' HP or a free weapon (a set one, or one drawn when the event comes up with a tier that fits the week; the result names it). It comes first: the buildings stay shut and the day cannot end until it is answered, so every event has a choice that costs nothing the player might lack (no gold, no materials, nobody fit to send: a test checks every event); then the free day goes on with its action as usual. If an event brings the walls to 0, the city falls at once.
- The run is lost when the city reaches 0 or a boss wins.

### Infirmary

- **In battle**: a companion who drops to 0 HP is injured and out of that battle. They do not act; enemies never aim at them (single-target weights are shared among those standing; an attack on the whole line skips them); every effect on them ends and nothing new lands on them, damage over time included; white magic cannot reach them (a spell on one ally is refused, one on every ally skips them). Nothing in battle undoes an injury. The battle is lost when everyone fielded is injured. The injured still get their XP share of a win and learn what their grades teach.
- **After the battle**, won or lost, everyone injured goes to the Infirmary (`Run.infirmary()`, in the order they went in): they leave the line, cannot be fielded or drilled, and are skipped by Rest, the daily heal and events that touch companions' HP. Their weapon stays in their hand and their progress is untouched; a tavern hire's level still averages the whole roster.
- **The stay**: 4 days (`RunConfig.INFIRMARY_DAYS`; a trait can lengthen it: `Run.infirmary_days`). Injured on day D, they are away days D+1 to D+4 and come out at the start of day D+5 at full HP and with every spell's uses back, on the bench (not into the line). Rest does nothing for them and never shortens it.
- **Cure** (a free-day errand, "Cure": gold only, never the day's action, any number a day): one companion comes out at once, at full HP and with every spell's uses back, onto the bench, and can be fielded the same day. It costs the days still to serve x (4 + 2 x their level) gold (`RunConfig.CURE_GOLD_PER_DAY_BASE`, `CURE_GOLD_PER_DAY_PER_LEVEL`): with 4 days left (the day after the injury) 24 gold at level 1, 56 at level 5, 96 at level 10, 136 at level 15; a level-10 companion with 2 days left costs 48. Days still to serve are the days until they would come out by themselves: 4 the day after the injury, 1 on the last day.
- **Nobody to fight**: on a battle or boss day with everyone in the Infirmary, the day can be given up (`Run.yield_day()`, allowed only then). It counts as a lost battle at the worst front: the city health that front would cost; on a boss day the city falls.

### Training ground

A free-day action (`Run.FreeAction.TRAIN`, "Train"), sharing the day's one action with Rest, Repair and Scavenge: one a day, not on battle or boss days, not before the day's event is answered.

- **The week's drills** (`Run.drills`), posted with the weekly restock: always **Sparring**, plus two proficiency drills, each a different weapon type or magic school drawn at random from all seven subjects (the five weapon types and the two schools), skipping only one in which the whole roster is already at S, so a posted drill is never useless to everyone. With fewer such types, fewer are posted (with none, only Sparring). Weapon drills are listed before school drills. A drill is not used up: the same one can be taken on every free day of the week.
- **Taking one**: pick a drill and a companion in the roster, fielded or on the bench. It costs the day's action and 30 gold (`RunConfig.DRILL_COST`). A proficiency drill can be taken by anyone below S in its weapon type or school, including someone at E with no points: that is how a new weapon type or school is opened (reaching E+ teaches an art or a spell like any grade, so a fighter can become a caster this way: the spell is cast with their MAG, however little, and Rest restores its uses like any other); Sparring needs a level below 20. `Run.drill_block_reason(drill, companion)` says why not; `Run.train(drill, companion)` returns a `DrillResult`. If nobody could learn from any posted drill, the action itself is blocked.
- **Quality** is rolled: poor 25%, good 50%, great 25% (`RunConfig.DRILL_QUALITY_WEIGHTS`).
- **Proficiency drill**: poor 3, good 5, great 8 points toward the next grade (`RunConfig.DRILL_POINTS`), never past S, with no per-battle cap. Good is about one battle's worth: a companion gains 4.8 points a battle on average with their weapon (capped at 8). Every grade reached teaches a random art (weapon) or spell (school) with the same rules as after a battle, right away (a drill is already after the fact); an art is carried only if a slot is free.
- **Sparring**: good = half an even share of the day's danger-2 battle XP pool: pool / 4 / 2 (on day 1: 86 / 8 = 11 XP; day 41: 23); poor = 60% of good, great = 150% (`RunConfig.DRILL_XP_PERCENT`), rounded. Level-ups are rolled as after a battle, and max HP gained raises HP.
- The result (`DrillResult`) carries the drill, the companion, the quality and its word, the gold paid, points gained, grade before and after with each grade reached (and the art or spell it taught), XP gained, and the level-ups in the `BattleReport.level_ups` shape plus the level each rose from and the stats before it.

### Scavenging

A free-day action (`Run.FreeAction.SCAVENGE`, "Scavenge"), sharing the day's one action with Rest, Repair and Train: not on battle or boss days, not before the day's event is answered, and not with nobody fit to go ("Nobody is fit to go.").

- **The day's sites** (`Run.scavenge_sites()`, a `ScavengeSite` each): every free day, when it begins, 2 places beyond the walls are rolled (`RunConfig.SCAVENGE_SITES_PER_DAY`), with different names (thirteen in all: The Burnt Mill, The Drowned Quarter...). None on battle or boss days. Each has a **size** (1 small, 2 medium, 3 large) and a **risk** (low, medium, high), rolled independently, each 40 / 35 / 25 (`SCAVENGE_SIZE_WEIGHTS`, `SCAVENGE_RISK_WEIGHTS`). If everyone available when the day began could not fill either, the first is rolled again with a size that fits, so one site can always be filled when anyone is fit.
- **Places**: one per size point, each testing a different stat, with its role: SPD Scout, STR Breaker, BLD Hauler, DEX Picker, LCK Finder, DEF Guard, RES Ward, MAG Seer. Its **target** is what a typical companion has in that stat on that day: the fourteen companions' average base stat + their average growth rate x (the typical level - 1), the typical level being 1 + 0.27 x (day - 1) (about 5 on day 14, 9 on day 28, 12 on day 42: the automated runs' pace), rounded (`SCAVENGE_STAT_BASE`, `SCAVENGE_STAT_GROWTH`, `SCAVENGE_LEVEL_PER_DAY`; `Run.scavenge_target`); then low risk -1, medium +0, high +2 (`SCAVENGE_RISK_TARGET_SHIFT`). At medium risk: day 1 SPD 8, STR 7, BLD 6, DEX 9, LCK 5, DEF 4, RES 5, MAG 4; day 42 SPD 13, STR 11, BLD 6 (it never grows), DEX 14, LCK 10, DEF 8, RES 9, MAG 7. A companion's stat is their base plus level-up gains (not their weapon's).
- **The haul** (a full one), fixed when the site is rolled and shown: **gold** 40% of the day's danger-1 battle reward (grown by `REWARD_GROWTH_PER_DAY`), x 1 / 1.8 / 2.6 by size and x 1 / 1.5 / 2.2 by risk (day 1: 16 small and low, 92 large and high; day 42: 30 and 170); **materials** 0-1 / 1-2 / 2-3 pieces by size, each timber or iron ore at random; a **weapon** with 0% / 10% / 25% by risk, drawn like an event's random weapon (any type); and **XP** to each sent, 30% of an even share of the day's danger-1 battle pool with a full line (4 on day 1, 9 on day 42). Numbers: `SCAVENGE_GOLD_*`, `SCAVENGE_MATERIALS_BY_SIZE`, `SCAVENGE_WEAPON_CHANCE`, `SCAVENGE_XP_SHARE`.
- **Sending a party** (`Run.scavenge(site, party)`, `party` indexed by place): every place filled ("Fill every place."), each by a different companion of the company not in the Infirmary, from the line or the bench. `Run.scavenge_block_reason(site, party)` says why not.
- **The chance** of a full haul (`Run.scavenge_chance`, shown before committing, also for a party still being chosen): 60 / 45 / 30 by risk, plus the average of the places' scores (`Run.scavenge_slot_score`): meeting the target +15 and +3 a point above, at most +30; short of it -6 a point, at most -30; an empty place -30. Rounded, then held to 25-95 (`SCAVENGE_BASE_CHANCE`, `SCAVENGE_MEET` and the others).
- **The outcome**, one roll of 1-100: at or under the chance a **full haul**; otherwise half the time **half a haul** (half the gold and XP, materials halved rounded down, no weapon), else **nothing** (`SCAVENGE_HALF_CHANCE`). Low risk costs nothing more. Medium risk: on nothing everyone sent loses 25% of max HP (rounded up, never below 1 HP; `SCAVENGE_HP_LOSS_FRACTION`). High risk: on half a haul everyone sent loses 25%; on nothing the one furthest short of their place's target (a tie, or nobody short: one of them at random) is injured and goes to the Infirmary exactly as after a battle, and the others lose 25%.
- XP brings level-ups as after a battle (max HP gained raises HP). The result (`ScavengeResult`) carries the site, the party, the chance and the roll, the outcome, the gold, materials and weapon brought (already in the run and the armory), who was injured, and for each one sent (`members`) the XP gained, HP lost, level and stats before, and the level-ups in the `DrillResult.level_ups` shape.

### Traits

A trait is something a companion is (`TraitData`; `RunCompanion.traits`, the same list in `CompanionProgress.traits` and so in battle): rolled when they first appear and never changed. The design, with every trait's wording and number: `design/traits.md`. Traits are built in waves; only those in the library (`TraitLibrary`, `data/traits/trait_library.tres`) are ever handed out. **Live now (waves 1 and 2, 139 traits):** wave 1, every trait that is a number with a condition: all of weapon hands (25: Sure, Heavy, Keen, Easy, Apt for each weapon), schools of magic (8), body and mind (11), two-edged (22) and the line (13); of tempo the ten damage and Hit lines (First Blood, Patient, Closer, Opener, Giant-Bane, Fowler, Spell-Breaker, Grudge, Counter-Eye, Kingslayer); of learning the ten Growing traits, Early Bloomer and Late Bloomer. Wave 2, the single moments: the rest of tempo (8), all of staying alive (12), the rest of learning (Quick Study, Glory-Hound, Bench Learner, Yard Rat, Second Trade, Apprentice, Deep Well), and of the city all but the three scavenging traits (11). Not yet: the company, Pathfinder, Sure-Footed and Pack Mule.

- **Handing out** (`Run._roll_traits`, numbers in `RunConfig.TRAIT_*`): 1, 2 or 3 traits at 75 / 22 / 3%; each drawn by rarity, common 10, uncommon 5, rare 1; never two of the same line of a family (Sword: Sure and Axe: Sure share the line "Sure"; Black: Deep and White: Deep share "Deep"; the ten Growing traits share one; every other trait is a line of its own), never two two-edged ones. No trait is on two people in a run. A trait tied to a weapon type or school goes to someone who can already use it (a grade above E in it) 4 times in 5: when one is drawn for someone who cannot, 80% of the time it is swapped for one of the same line they can use (or any tied one they can); the fifth time it stays where it fell. The two starters roll theirs when the run begins; each tavern offer rolls when the week's offers are rolled (`Run.offer_traits(companion)` shows them before hiring) and a hire keeps them; an offer nobody hired gives its traits back to the pool when the week ends. All with the run's generator.
- **Words**: Hit and Crit are percentage points on the chance to hit or to strike critically (added before the chance is held to its limits); damage is added to each blow after DEF or RES (and an effective strike's half again), before a critical doubles it, never below 1; Avoid +10 means enemies have 10 less Hit against them; drawn more / less multiplies how often enemies aim at them. Places in the line are counted as in the barracks: the rear is the leftmost, the front the rightmost; a line of one is both; middle is neither. "Their first action", "the first three", "from the fourth on" count the companion's own actions in the battle (Guard and Focus included). "Neighbours" are those either side in the line, "behind" the one to their left; such a trait works while its holder is standing.
- **Where they count**, always through the same functions the screens read:
  - Plain stat bonuses (Hale, Strong, Ox...) are in `CompanionProgress.stats()` (so max HP, stamina, the Attack in the barracks and smithy, the weight penalty and scavenging all include them); `trait_stat_bonus(stat)` says how much of a stat comes from traits. A stat bonus that needs the battle (Proud, Vanguard, Lone Blade, Last Stand, Sprinter's Speed) is added by `CompanionUnit.stat` in battle only.
  - Hit, Crit, damage and cannot-miss: in `Battle.forecast` and `Battle.spell_forecast` (so the forecast shows them); weapon-hand traits count with that weapon type in hand (attacks and arts), school traits with that school's spells, the rest with every strike, black spells included. Avoid and the damage a companion takes from enemy blows (Anchor, Shieldmate, Sidestep, Wall): `Battle.enemy_forecast`. Aim: `Battle.target_weights`.
  - Art stamina (Easy, Hothead, Thrifty): `Battle.art_cost(c, art)`, never below 0. Spell uses (Deep): `CompanionProgress.max_uses(spell)`, which Rest refills to. Heals (Kind): `Battle.heal_amount`; on one target (Faithless): `Battle.heal_amount_on`. Lasting: a white spell's buff they cast lasts 1 turn longer.
  - Apt (+50% points): weapon points in battle by half again, fractions carried to the next action, and the battle's cap (`Battle.proficiency_cap`) half again (8 to 12); magic points 4 to 6 a cast, cap 12 to 18 (`Battle.magic_cap`); a drill of that type or school, `Run.drill_points` (good: 5 to 8, rounded).
  - Growth: `Levels.growth_rate_of(progress, stat)` (Growing +15, Early and Late Bloomer by the level being reached: up to 8, or 9 and above), used by every level-up; `Levels.growth_rate` stays the companion's own.
  - XP (Thin-Blooded +15%, Quick Study +10%): every share of a battle, Sparring (`Run.drill_xp_for`) and scavenging, rounded down (`Levels.with_traits`); a report's XP is what they got. The Infirmary stay (`Run.infirmary_days`; the cure price follows the days left) and the day's-end heal (`Run.day_heal`, Daredevil half).
- **Single moments (wave 2)**, each where the moment happens:
  - In battle: Focus (`Battle.focus_amount`, Steady Breath); after each of their actions (Tireless: +1 stamina, never past full); an art's recovery (`Battle.art_recovery`, Quick Hands on their first art of the battle); felling an enemy (Bloodrush stamina, Blood-Drinker HP, Glory-Hound's kill points counted twice towards the XP share, Relentless: that action has no recovery); a critical hit (Staggering Blow: the target's next action 1 turn later, refused once that action was already delayed, as for spells); the first time they fall below half Health (Second Wind: stamina full); a blow that would fell them (Clings to Life: left at 1, once a battle; damage over time is not a blow); enemy blows (`Battle.enemy_forecast`: Thick Hide physical -1, Spell-Scarred magic -1, Brace half for the first blow that lands on them, Stubborn Avoid +15 at 5 Health or less); damage over time (Clean Blood: none); a harmful effect landing on them (Calm: 1 turn shorter, at least 1); white magic on them (Good Patient, `Battle.heal_amount_on`).
  - After a battle: Field Dresser heals 15% of max HP if won and still standing; Looter +10 gold if won, Bounty Hunter +4 gold for each enemy they felled if won (like a weapon's gold for kills); Woodsman +1 timber and Prospector +1 iron ore for everyone who fought, won or lost; Night Watch, on the bench, takes 3 off the walls' loss of a lost battle. All in the report: `BattleReport.trait_gold` (in `gold`), `materials`, `city_damage`, and one line each in `trait_lines` ("Elena, Looter: +10 gold").
  - The day's end (`Run.day_heal`): Fast Healer heals 35% instead of 20% and Camp Cook gives everyone 5 percentage points more; percentage points add up (with the Inn's 30%, Fast Healer heals 45%), then Daredevil halves.
  - Learning: Yard Rat (a poor drill comes out good for them); Second Trade (+50% points in the weapon type of their second-best grade, by points, a tie going to the type listed first, in battle and drills); Apprentice (arts and spells are drawn as if the grade were one letter higher); Deep Well (+1 use of every spell); Bench Learner (a quarter of an even share of a won battle's XP from the bench, `BattleReport.reserve_xp`).
  - The city's traits work while their holder is in the company and not in the Infirmary, line or bench: the smithy's price (`Run.shop_price(item)`, Haggler -10%), a hire (`Run.hire_cost()`, Well-Liked -20), a drill (`Run.drill_cost()`, Drillmaster -10), a repair (`Run.repair_amount()`, Mason's Kin +5), an event choice that costs gold (`Run.choice_gold(choice)`, Silver Tongue -20%). Several holders add up. Healer's Favourite halves their own cure (`Run.cure_cost`).
  - **With the city's upgrades** they add together, with the floors stated: a hire 100 - 25 (Tavern level 2) - 20 (Well-Liked) = 55, never below 0; a repair 30 (Mason's Yard level 1) + 5 = 35; the overnight heal 30% (Inn level 1) + 15 = 45%; the stay 3 (Infirmary level 1) - 1 = 2 days, never below 1; the cure price -25% (Infirmary level 2) - 50% = a quarter, never below 0; the reserve's XP a quarter (Barracks level 1) + a quarter (Bench Learner) = half an even share; Yard Rat and the Drill Yard's level 1 do the same thing, so together they are no more than either.

### The city's upgrades

Timber builds the city, iron ore forges arms. Both come from battles won (a share of fronts), the bosses (3 of each), events and scavenging; `Run.materials_taken` keeps the count. Measured over the 200 automated runs before upgrades existed, per run still going that week: week 1 about 3 timber and 3 ore, week 2 6 and 6 (the first boss), week 3 3 and 3, week 4 5 and 5 (the second boss), week 5 2 and 2, week 6 1 and 1.5; a run that reaches day 42 takes in about 22 timber and 23 iron ore.

- **Repairs** (the day's action, at the Mason's Yard) cost 3 timber and no ore for 20 city health (`Run.repair_cost`, `Run.repair_amount`).
- **Swap** at the Mason's Yard: 2 of one material for 1 of the other, any number of times (`Run.swap_block_reason(from, to)`, `Run.swap(from, to)`; `RunConfig.SWAP_GIVE`, `SWAP_GET`).
- **Nine facilities**, two levels each, level 1 before level 2 (`Run.Facility`: Watchtower, Smithy, Tavern, Inn, Infirmary, Drill Yard, Barracks, Mason's Yard, Gate; `Run.facility_level`, `Run.facility_lines(f, level)` for each level's line). The Smithy and the Gate are paid in iron ore, the rest in timber: level 1 costs 3, level 2 costs 5 (`RunConfig.UPGRADE_COST`, `FACILITY_MATERIAL`; `Run.upgrade_cost(f)`). Set so the automated player, taking materials whenever offered and repairing first, builds about 6 of the 18 in a run (`tests/run_policy.gd`).
- Upgrading, swapping, enchanting and moving an enchantment are errands: free days once the day's event is answered, any number, never the day's action (`Run.upgrade_block_reason(f)`, `Run.upgrade(f)`). An upgrade works from the moment it is paid for.

| Facility | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Watchtower | The boss days and which boss comes are known | Tomorrow's fronts are known a day early |
| Smithy | Opens enchanting | An enchantment comes out +2 twice as often |
| Tavern | One more hire offered each week | Hires cost 25 gold less |
| Inn | Everyone heals 30% overnight instead of 20% | Every spell gets one use back each night |
| Infirmary | Stays are 3 days instead of 4 | Cures cost a quarter less |
| Drill Yard | Drills never come out poor | A third drill is posted each week |
| Barracks | The reserve gets a quarter share of a battle's XP | The reserve heals fully overnight |
| Mason's Yard | Repairs mend 30 instead of 20 | The walls hold 120 instead of 100 |
| Gate | A third scavenging site each day | A lost front costs the walls 5 less |

- **Watchtower**: without level 1 the run does not tell which days ahead are boss days or which boss comes: `Run.boss_days_known()` is false, `Run.upcoming()` and `Run.seen_day_type(d)` show a boss day ahead as a battle day, `Run.boss_on(d)` is null for a day ahead and `Run.boss_days_ahead()` holds at most today (the boss day itself, and its boss, are seen when it comes). Level 2: on every day before a battle or boss day, tomorrow's fronts are rolled when the day begins (or when level 2 is bought) and `Run.tomorrow_fronts()` returns them; they are the fronts that day brings.
- **Tavern** level 1: the week's offers are 2-3; bought mid-week, one more is offered at once (with their traits). Level 2: `Run.hire_cost()` is 75.
- **Inn** level 1: `Run.day_heal` uses 30%. Level 2: at every day's end each companion not in the Infirmary gets one use back of every spell they know, never past full.
- **Infirmary** level 1: `Run.infirmary_days` is 3 (traits still add); stays already running end a day sooner (not before tomorrow). Level 2: `Run.cure_cost` x 0.75, rounded.
- **Drill Yard** level 1: a poor drill comes out good. Level 2: three proficiency drills a week; bought mid-week, one more is posted at once.
- **Barracks** level 1: after a won battle everyone on the bench gets a quarter of an even share (pool / 4 / 4, rounded; traits apply), in `BattleReport.reserve_xp`, level-ups in `level_ups`. Level 2: at the day's end everyone on the bench is at full HP.
- **Mason's Yard** level 1: a repair mends 30. Level 2: `Run.max_city_health()` is 120 (repairs and events go up to it).
- **Gate** level 1: three scavenging sites a day; bought on a free day, a third is rolled at once. Level 2: `Run.front_city_damage(danger)` is 10 / 15 / 20 (fronts rolled after, and tomorrow's if already rolled).

### Enchanting

At the Smithy once it has its level 1 (an errand, see above). Any weapon the company owns, in hand or in the armory (`OwnedWeapon`).

- **An attempt** (`Run.enchant(w)` returns an `EnchantResult`: the level before, levels gained, the stat of each in order, whether the double was rolled, the cost) adds +1, or +2 with 20% (40% with the Smithy's level 2; `RunConfig.ENCHANT_DOUBLE_PERCENT`) when the cap leaves room for both. Going from +n costs n + 1 iron ore and 20 x (n + 1) gold (`Run.enchant_cost(w)`: materials and "gold"): +0 to +3 is 6 ore and 120 gold. `Run.enchant_block_reason(w)` says why not.
- **Each level is +1 to one stat** drawn at random from all eleven (Health, Stamina, Strength, Magic, Critical, Dexterity, Speed, Defence, Resistance, Luck, Build) while the weapon is held; the same stat can come up again, and some are duds. `OwnedWeapon.enchant_level`, `enchant_stats` (stat -> amount), `title()` ("Iron Sword +2"), `enchant_lines()` ("+1 Strength").
- **The cap** (`Run.enchant_cap(w)`) is by tier: wooden +1, bronze +2, iron +3, steel +4, silver and platinum +5; a rare weapon +4 (`RunConfig.ENCHANT_CAP_BY_TIER`, `ENCHANT_RARE_CAP`).
- **Held**, the enchantment is in the holder's stats (`CompanionProgress.stats()`, so max HP, the Attack, the weight penalty, scavenging and the battle all include it); `CompanionProgress.enchant_stat_bonus(stat)` says how much of a stat comes from it. Put down, it stays with the weapon (HP is held to the new max). `RunCompanion.attack_with(owned_weapon)` gives the Attack with another owned weapon, its enchantment included.
- **Moving an enchantment** (`Run.move_enchant(from, to)`, `move_enchant_block_reason`, `move_enchant_cost()`): 2 iron ore moves a weapon's whole enchantment to another owned weapon of the same type that has none; levels above the new weapon's cap are lost, the last ones gained first.

### Battles

- The company is the row of portraits along the bottom; the rightmost stands nearest the enemies and is aimed at most. The small roundel on each portrait is how often the enemy aims at that place. Someone who has just acted is dimmed under an hourglass with the number of actions until they are ready again, the same picture an enemy's countdown uses; someone injured is reddened and sunk. Click anyone who is ready to choose who acts: they step up out of the line, and their portrait fills the left edge of the screen.
- Give an order from the list beside them, or press its number: Attack, the companion's equipped combat arts (up to 3), Magic (for anyone who knows a spell), Guard, Focus (restores 5 stamina, about a tier 1 art's cost). Point at an order to read what it does under the list; an art you cannot afford is greyed out and says why.
- Magic changes the list to the companion's spells, each with its school, tier and uses left (number keys; Back, Esc or right-click returns; more than six spells page with More). A spell with no uses left is greyed out and says why.
- For Attack and single-target arts, point at an enemy to see hit %, damage and crit %, then click it to strike. Arts that strike every enemy light up every enemy at once; click any of them to confirm. Right-click or Esc backs out.
- A spell aimed at a companion is cast by clicking their portrait below; each portrait shows what it would do (for a heal, HP before and after). A spell for every ally or every enemy lights them all up; one click confirms.
- Under each enemy: its HP, an hourglass with the turns left until it acts, and the kind of action coming. Every action you take, art, spell or not, is one turn (the code and the data files call a turn a `tick`). A pending action already pushed back by a spell shows its countdown in pale blue with a DELAYED tag; one brought forward by another enemy's order shows it in orange with a HASTENED tag (and pops SOONER -N).
- Traits such as FLYING show as a tag above an enemy's name (hover it). The forecast says EFFECTIVE when the strike deals half as much again, and MAGIC when it is against RES.
- Lasting effects show on the unit with the turns left (on party panels under the status tag; poison and burning in green-yellow). Poison pops its damage at the end of each turn.
- A reflecting stance wraps the enemy in a ring of pink thorns with a REFLECTS N% tag; the forecast turns pink and says what striking costs you (normal and critical). Spells pass through.
- An enemy that aims from the left carries an AIMS LEFT tag. The party panels' "targeted N%" then counts it: with only such enemies the figure is the mirrored one ("aims from the left"); with both kinds the panel shows both ("targeted N% · from the left N%").
- A second phase gets the big banner (PHASE TWO, its name and one line) and a tag under the enemy. Recovery added by an enemy pops +N RECOVERY on the panel; an enemy's heal pops +N on it.
- When nobody is ready, Wait (1 or Space) lets a turn pass. L opens the battle log. Clicking during playback speeds it up.
- Hold Shift while pointing at an enemy or a companion for what is known about them: stats, grades, arts, traits and what is on them now. Let go of Shift, or move off them, and the sheet goes. Nothing of the kind pops up on hover alone.

Proficiency and arts:

- Each companion has a grade in every weapon type: E, E+, D, D+, C, C+, B, B+, A, A+, S. Attacking gives 1 point in the type of the weapon held, a combat art 2; Guard, Focus and Wait give none. One companion can gain at most 8 points per battle. The grade and progress for the weapon held show under the companion's name on the left.
- Every grade reached teaches one random art of that weapon type the companion does not know yet (higher grades roll higher tiers). In battle the grade rises at once and a banner announces it, but the art is learned only when the battle is over (won or lost, by everyone fielded, injured ones too), in the order the grades were reached, so nothing new can be used in the battle it was earned in. It is carried (equipped) if one of the three slots is free; otherwise it is known and left behind, and can be swapped for a carried one (`CompanionProgress.swap`). `Battle.learn_after_battle()` does this; the battle calls it itself when it ends.
- Weapons need a grade to be held: wooden E, bronze D, iron C, steel B, silver A, platinum S (see Weapons); two-proficiency weapons also need a grade one lower (at least E+) in a second type. Most companions start at D in their own weapon, so bronze is their first upgrade.
- After a battle the result comes in four beats, and a click, Space or Enter moves it on. First: won or lost, what it gave (gold, materials, XP) or cost the walls, and the company in a row of cards with their XP bars filling, each by their own share; a card shows the level, the XP they got, a skull on the face for every enemy they felled, gold marks beside the name of whoever earned the most contribution points (when one companion stands out), weapon and magic grades with what the battle added, anything learned, and INJURED across the face of anyone bound for the Infirmary (hover a card for the exact numbers). Then every level gained is shown on its own: the companion stands beside their ten stats and a light goes down the list one stat at a time; where it stops, the number goes up. The gains were already rolled when the XP was granted; the screen only reveals them in order. Then every combat art or spell learned is shown on its own, the companion beside it: its name, what it costs in large figures (stamina, or a spell's uses, and Recovery: how many of their next actions they sit out) and what it does, one thing to a line. An art that found a free place among the three carried is shown alone, with a line saying which place it took; so is a spell. When all three places were taken the art has been left behind and the panel becomes a comparison: the new art's card beside the cards of the three carried, each written out the same way (figures, then one line for each effect). Clicking a carried art leaves that one behind and carries the new one in its place; clicking it again, or the new art, puts things back. The comparison moves on only by its own "Go on", so a stray click cannot skip the choice. Last, the cards again and the way back to the city. The Drill Yard shows a level or an art gained from a drill the same way. During a battle a grade reached only raises a banner naming the grade.

### Magic

- Two schools: **Black Magic** (offence) and **White Magic** (support). Every companion has a grade in each, with the same grades and thresholds as weapons. Casting gives 4 points in the spell's school, up to 12 magic points per battle (separate from the weapon cap). Every school grade reached teaches a random spell of that school not yet known, with the same tier odds, no-duplicate rule and fall-through as combat arts, and like an art it is learned when the battle is over, not during it. All known spells can be cast; there are no slots. Each school has 12 spells, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/spells/`, all listed in `spell_library.tres`). A mage's signature spell is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other mage of that school can learn it.
- A spell costs one turn and no stamina, then a recovery of its tier (1-3). Each spell has a number of uses per companion (6 / 4 / 2 by tier, set per spell); uses last between battles and days and only Rest restores them.
- Black spells: damage = caster MAG + spell Mt - target RES (at least 1), with the normal accuracy and crit formulas using the spell's own Hit. They may strike every enemy, ignore part of RES, debuff the target (lowered stats, taking more damage, or damage every turn, which can kill: Blight), and delay the target's pending action: a pending action can be delayed only once; the next one it picks can be delayed again. Combat arts never touch countdowns.
- White spells always succeed: heals (caster MAG + the spell's power, on anyone still standing; an injured companion cannot be healed or reached by white magic at all), buffs, less damage taken, a veil (enemies aim elsewhere) or a beacon (enemies aim here), a reflecting ward (Thornward: like an art's stance, an enemy whose physical action lands on the ally is hurt for a share of the damage and can die of it; magic is never answered), and removing debuffs, on one ally or all.
- Enemy actions are physical (STR against DEF) or magic (MAG against RES); Guard halves both. The Goblin Wizard, the casters of the bestiary, the Rimeclaw Wyvern's frost, the Oathbreaker's Black Tide, and the Faceless Princess, Forsaken Seraph and Eclipse Sovereign are magic. Enemy MAG grows with the day like STR.
- **Magic weapons** are rare variants of the normal types: Attack and arts with them use MAG in place of STR in their type's formula (see Weapons), against RES, with the weapon's own Mt.
- **Effectiveness**: enemies carry traits (Flying and Large). Weapons, arts and spells list the traits they are effective against; every bow is effective against Flying and every spear against Large. An effective strike or spell deals half as much damage again: 50% of the damage left after DEF or RES, rounded down, added before a critical doubles it and before Guard or other damage-taken multipliers. Effective through both weapon and art still counts once. The share is `BattleConfig.EFFECTIVE_DAMAGE_BONUS_PERCENT`.
- The Barracks shows the whole company and everything about whoever is chosen on one screen. Down the left is the company: the line from the rear to the front, the bench, and whoever lies in the Infirmary (dimmed, with the days left); a row is carried to another place or to the bench, and the up and down keys go through everyone. The rest is the chosen companion's sheet, in three columns. First: a portrait, level and health, their stats (what traits add to a number is written small before it), then under its own heading how readily each stat grows (a figure and a line each, traits counted), their traits (each by name with what it gives, what a two-edged one takes in red, and the rarity of an uncommon or rare one), and at the foot the three orders that move them in the line, written as one line of words to click. Second: the weapon in hand and the Attack it gives, the armory (what they could take first, each with the Attack it would give; then what they cannot hold and what others hold, with the reason), and their grade in every weapon and both schools with a line for the way to the next. Third: every art they know (click one to carry it into battle or leave it behind; three are carried) and every known spell with uses left; what the art or spell under the pointer costs and does is set out at the foot of the column (figures, then one line for each effect). The battle orders do the same under the list for the art or spell under the pointer, with a line first when it cannot be used now (the stamina it needs, or the weapon). Everywhere a recovery is written it is "Recovery 1" (sits out the next action), "Recovery 2", and so on.
- The war table (the Gate, on battle and boss days) is about the enemy: the scouts' reports lie side by side, drawn as large as the table allows. Along its foot, small, stands the line as it will march, from the rear to the front, each place marked with the share of the enemy's single blows it draws (7%, 13%, 27% and 53% with four in the line and nothing else changing the aim) and an arrow toward the enemy. The order of the line can be changed there: carry someone onto another's place, or click them and then the place; Left and Right move whoever is picked up. Who marches at all, their weapons and their arts are the Barracks' business; the reserve and the Infirmary are not shown here. With nobody in the line the table says so and the reports cannot be marched on.
- The Drill Yard is a fenced ring of sand in the square. Inside, the company is listed down the left, the one chosen stands in the yard, and the sergeant's board on the right carries the week's three notices. Each notice shows where the chosen companion stands (their grade or level, with the line toward the next) and how far a poor, good or great drill would carry them, or why they cannot take it. The slip at the foot sets them to it. How it went is then written up on the board: a poor, good or great drill, the line filling by what it gave, the new grade and anything learned; a level gained from sparring is shown stat by stat as after a battle. The yard can be entered on any free day once the gate's matter is answered, to read the notices.
- The Infirmary is the whitewashed house with the red cross in the square, and its door is open on any day. Inside there is a bed for everyone injured: who lies in it, the days of the stay as marks (those served filled in), the days to go and the day they are up, and a slip that pays the healer (the price, or why not today). In the city the injured show dimmed at the end of the company's row, and clicking one opens the Infirmary; the morning news names whoever came out. In battle an injured companion carries no "targeted" figure and cannot be picked for a white spell. On a battle or boss day with nobody on their feet the Gate offers a letter, "Nobody Can March Out", that gives the day up. The end of a run lists who is still standing and who is in the Infirmary.

In the Smithy the price list is on the right and the company on the left: point at a weapon and each companion shows whether they could hold it and what their Attack would become (and whether it is too heavy). A weapon just bought lies on a slip over the company ("Who takes it?"): everyone who can hold it lights up with what their Attack would become, and a click hands it to them, their old weapon going back to the armory; "To the armory" keeps it for the Barracks instead.

The words are in the tooltips.

## Bestiary

Every normal enemy belongs to a band (`EnemyData.band`): early in weeks 1-2, middle in weeks 3-4, late in weeks 5-6 (`RunConfig.BAND_FIRST_DAY`: days 1, 15, 29). Its groups never appear before the band begins, and fronts favour the band of the day (`RunConfig.BAND_AGE_WEIGHT`: older groups still turn up, scaled to the day). Danger 3 fronts from the middle band on can field four enemies.

- **Early (weeks 1-2)**: Orc, Goblin Thief, Goblin Wizard, Stalker, Skeleton Bird*, Winged Zombie*, Slime Monster (soaks blows, re-forms, slows), Briarwing* (thorn mantle reflects 50%).
- **Middle (weeks 3-4)**: Cursed Warrior, Armored Beast, Minotaur, Blackwing* (fast diver), Poisoned Wings* (poisons the line), Flame Eagle* (fire caster, burning), Poison Spawn (heavy poison, corrodes DEF/RES), One-Eye* (debuffer, its gaze adds recovery), Ancient Treant (protector: buffs and heals every enemy).
- **Late (weeks 5-6)**: Stormcrest* (lightning caster), Void Raven* (its caw orders the others to act sooner), Shadowed Wings* (assassin that aims from the left), Faceless Spawn (burner), Cursed Dweller (slow heavy hitter, stacking frenzy), Forgotten Guardian* (wards every enemy, frost aegis reflects).

\* Flying. Large (spears are effective): Orc, Armored Beast, Minotaur, Ancient Treant, Cursed Dweller, and the bosses Mossback Troll, Rampart Golem and The Horned Tyrant. Each enemy's data is in `data/enemies/`, its groups in `data/run/groups/`.

## Bosses

`data/enemies/bosses/` and `data/run/bosses/` (a boss group: the boss, then any escorts, who grow with the day; `description` is the one-line warning). Each boss day draws one from its pool by the run's seed (`Run.boss_draw`). Every boss has one idea:

- **Day 14**: The Horned Tyrant (its War Bellow stacks: it grows stronger the longer the fight runs), Mossback Troll (regenerates 18% of its HP on every Buff it shows: burst it down), Rampart Golem (high DEF, no RES, slow and crushing, braces to halve damage: magic, DEF-ignoring arts and Guard).
- **Day 28**: Rimeclaw Wyvern (flies, frost magic, Take Wing makes it very hard to hit: bows and accuracy), The Oathbreaker (its Oath of Thorns, a Defend, reflects 80% of weapon and art damage: read the intent, use spells), The Fallen Commander (with a One-Eye and a Flame Eagle: rallies every enemy and orders them to act sooner; alone it makes a last stand: choose the kill order).
- **Day 42**: The Faceless Princess (aims from the left and poisons: put the sturdiest on the left), The Forsaken Seraph (flies, magic, charges Judgement on everyone: RES, bows, speed and a delay spell), The Eclipse Sovereign (adds recovery to everyone; at half HP turns faster and harsher).

### Enemy mechanics

All in the battle rules and driven by data (`EnemyActionData`, `EnemyData`, `StatusEffectData`):
- **Damage over time** (`damage_per_tick`): at the end of every turn; Guard does not halve it. It can injure a companion, and ends with the injury. White magic that removes debuffs clears it.
- **Reflection** (`reflect_percent`): a weapon strike or combat art that lands on the bearer deals that share of its damage (at least 1) back to the striker. Spells are exempt.
- **Healing** (`heal_percent`) on the enemy itself, or on every enemy with the ALL_ALLIES targeting (which also spreads buffs).
- **Reversed targeting** (`EnemyData.reversed_targeting` or per action): the position weights mirrored.
- **Orders** (`hasten_allies`): every other enemy's countdown drops by N, never below 1.
- **Recovery pressure** (`add_recovery`): adds to the recovery of each companion reached. A lone companion still ignores recovery.
- **Phases** (`phase_trigger`: at an HP share, or when the last one standing): a second action list, a banner, an optional lasting effect, and the pending action is replaced.
- Also: stacking effects (`max_stacks`) and actions never picked twice in a row (`no_repeat`).

Exit code 0 means every test passed. `TEST_ONLY=playthrough` runs only tests whose name contains that text (several, comma-separated), `TEST_SKIP=playthrough` skips them; `TEST_VERBOSE=1` prints each test's name. Besides the battle rules (`run_tests.gd`), the run rules (`run_rule_tests.gd`), magic and effectiveness (`magic_tests.gd`), the bestiary, bosses and their mechanics (`bestiary_tests.gd`) the weapon rules (`weapon_tests.gd`: starting stats, the ladder, attack power by type, effectiveness, Large, where weapons come from, two-proficiency weapons) the training ground (`training_tests.gd`), the Infirmary (`infirmary_tests.gd`) and the rare weapons (`rare_weapon_tests.gd`), the run prints the smithy's stock by week, typical damage of every weapon type and tier against the day's average enemy at levels 1, 6 and 12, a random-policy battle win rate, how fast grades rise, and an automated playthrough: a simple policy (`run_policy.gd`) plays 200 whole runs through the rule classes and reports how often it wins, on which day runs end, and for each boss how often it was faced and beaten. The policy answers the mechanics in obvious ways: it never strikes into a reflecting stance, delays a long charged attack on everyone, guards a heavy blow aimed at it, and stands its sturdiest on the left against a boss that aims from the left. On a free day with nothing better to do (after resting, hiring, buying and mending) it trains: a drill in the second type of a two-proficiency weapon owned or in stock that a fielded companion lacks only that grade for and that would raise their Attack (the only time it opens a weapon type or school from E: a Runeblade's black magic, say), else a proficiency drill in the weapon a fielded companion holds (above E), else Sparring for the lowest-level fielded companion. It fields whoever is not in the Infirmary, hires while fewer than four are available, while the line is short cures those of its four highest-level companions it has the gold for who would otherwise miss a battle or boss day (the highest level first; curing, hiring and buying cost no action, so it still rests, mends or trains the same day), and gives a day up only when nobody can be fielded. With the day's action still unused it scavenges: before training when short of gold (under two drills' worth), else when it cannot train; it sends whoever gives each site its best chance and picks the site with the best expected haul (gold, materials and XP, less the cost of an injury on high risk), never high risk the day before a boss. The playthrough reports trips per run by outcome. At each free day's end it spends materials: it swaps 2 for 1 when one material is 8 or more and 4 above the other, and ore for timber while the walls are down 20 or more and the timber is short of a repair; builds the timber facilities in a fixed order (Infirmary, Inn, Mason's Yard, Barracks, Tavern, Drill Yard, then level 2 of the Infirmary and Inn, the Watchtower, and the other second levels) only while the walls are down 20 or less, keeping a repair's worth then; and spends ore on the Smithy, then the line's weapons up to +2 (keeping 60 gold), the Gate, the Smithy's second level, the line's weapons to their caps, the Gate's second level, each list stopping at the first thing it cannot pay for. Changing to a better weapon of the same type, it moves the enchantment over. The playthrough reports upgrades, enchant levels, moves and swaps per run, and the materials taken in.

## Layout

- `scripts/battle/` battle rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, seeded `RandomNumberGenerator` injected. `battle_config.gd` holds every tuning number (grade thresholds, points per action, the per-battle cap, the tier odds per grade). `proficiency.gd` is the grade rules (including whether a companion may equip a weapon), `art_learning.gd` picks the art learned on a grade-up.
- `scripts/run/` the run rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, every random choice from an injected seeded generator. `Run` (the day flow, city, gold and materials, roster and line, today's pending choice, the outcome; commands return false or a reason when not allowed), `RunCalendar` (the weekly pattern and where the events fall), `RunCompanion` (data, progress, HP between days), `Levels` (XP per level and the growth rolls), `RunInventory`/`OwnedWeapon` (the armory: each weapon held by at most one companion), `Front`, `BattleReport`, `Drill`/`DrillResult` (the training ground), and `run_config.gd` with every run tuning number (day counts, rewards, city damage, prices, enemy scaling). `CompanionProgress` is what a companion carries between battles: level, XP and stat gains, proficiency, known and equipped arts, and the weapon in hand; `Battle` reads and updates it.
- `scripts/data/` `Resource` classes for companions, enemies, weapons, combat arts, the art library, spells (`SpellData`) and the spell library, enemy traits (`EnemyTraits`), enemy actions and effects, companion traits (`TraitData`, its `TraitModifier`s, `TraitLibrary`; worked out by `scripts/battle/trait_rules.gd`). `scripts/battle/spell_learning.gd` picks the spell learned on a school grade-up; `CombatMath.DamageType` is physical or magic.
- `data/` the `.tres` data: companions (stats, growth rates, weapon, starting grades, signature art), enemies (with their action lists; bosses in `enemies/bosses/`), weapons (`<tier>_<type>.tres`: wooden, bronze, iron, steel, silver and platinum of each of the five types, the 24 two-proficiency weapons `<tier>_<family>.tres`, and the fourteen rare weapons, the four magic ones among them), `arts/<weapon type>/` combat arts, `arts/art_library.tres` (every art that can be learned, signature arts included), `spells/black/` and `spells/white/` spells with `spells/spell_library.tres` (every spell that can be learned, the mages' signature spells included), `battles/prototype_battle.tres` (used by the battle tests), and `run/`: `run_content.tres` lists everything a run is built from: the companions, front `sites/`, enemy `groups/` (danger and the days they appear), `bosses/` (one per boss day), `events/`, `weapons` (the 30-weapon ladder the smithy, events and bosses draw from) `hybrid_weapons` (the 24 two-proficiency weapons) and `rare_weapons` (the fourteen rare weapons). A new event, group or rare weapon is a new `.tres` added to that list; no code.
- `scripts/ui/`, `scenes/main.tscn` (the main scene). `game.gd` shows the title (`title_screen.gd`) and then the city. `city_screen.gd` is the hub: it reads the run, sets each building's state, and opens the war table (`war_table.gd`), the barracks (`party_screen.gd`), the smithy and tavern (`store_screen.gd`), the scavenging table (`scavenge_screen.gd`), letters (`letter_panel.gd`: visits, events, the watch's report, the opening), the battle (`battle_screen.gd`) and the run-end screen over itself. The battle screen plays a battle the run prepared (`Run.start_battle`), emits `finished`, and shows the `BattleReport` the run hands back. Screens only read run and battle state and call their commands.
- The look: things from the world rather than an interface. `ui_theme.gd` holds the colours, the fonts (a book serif; system fonts for now) and the shared drawing (framed boards with cut corners and grain, parchment sheets). `SlantPanel`, `SlantBar`, `ActionButton` and `RowButton` are the shared pieces (the names date from when panels leaned; `UiTheme.SLANT` is 0 now); the two buttons have a `paper` style for lines written on parchment. In battle, `action_menu.gd` is the close-up and the orders (light on a soft-edged veil of shadow, so the art and the field show through; `ActionButton`'s `veil` style) and `party_panel.gd` one portrait of the line; in the Barracks, `muster_row.gd` is one of the company in the list, which can be carried and set down. Companion art (`assets/character/`) is a cut-out with no background: wherever a face is shown in a frame or a ring the UI paints a plain ground behind it, and `CompanionData.figure_scale` evens out how large each was painted so heads match side by side (`portrait_focus` says where the face is). `ui_icons.gd` draws the small pictures. `art_facts.gd` sets out an art's or a spell's cost and effect to be read at a glance (the learned panel `learned_panel.gd`, the battle orders and the barracks sheet use it). `stat_table.gd` writes a companion's stats and, apart from them, their growth rates (the barracks sheet and a hire's paper use it); `store_screen.gd` the tavern and the smithy, and `training_screen.gd` the drill yard. After a battle, `battle_result.gd` runs the result (`spoils_card.gd` is one companion's card, `level_up_panel.gd` the stat-by-stat reveal of one level).
- The city is drawn in code, with no image files: `city_art.gd` is the kit (walls, roofs, windows, doors, flames), `city_building.gd` the nine buildings, `city_backdrop.gd` the scene and its times of day (`set_phase`), `interior_backdrop.gd` the inside of a building, `stage_backdrop.gd` the battlefield. Where each building stands is the `RECTS` table in `city_building.gd`. Painted art can replace any of these later without touching the screens.
- The layout is designed at 1920x1080 and scales with the window.
- `tests/` headless tests: `run_tests.gd` (battle rules, and the runner), `run_rule_tests.gd` (run rules and the playthrough), `run_policy.gd` (the automated player), `magic_tests.gd`, `bestiary_tests.gd`, `weapon_tests.gd`, `training_tests.gd`, `infirmary_tests.gd`, `rare_weapon_tests.gd`, `scavenge_tests.gd`, `trait_tests.gd`, `trait_hook_tests.gd` (the traits of single moments, and later waves), `city_tests.gd` (the city's upgrades, materials and enchanting).
- `data/traits/<family>/` one `.tres` per companion trait and `data/traits/trait_library.tres` listing those handed out; written by `tools/generate_traits.gd` (`godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/generate_traits.gd`); a trait can also be written by hand as a `.tres` and added to the library.
- `assets/` character and enemy art.

## Weapons

Six tiers, and every one of the five types (sword, axe, spear, bow, dagger) exists in every tier: 30 weapons (`WeaponData.tier`, `RunContent.weapons`). Mt = the tier's base (wooden 0, bronze 3, iron 6, steel 9, silver 12, platinum 15) + the type's offset (sword 0, spear +1, bow -1, dagger -2; the axe 0 at wooden and bronze, +1 at iron and steel, +2 at silver, +3 at platinum), never below 0 (`BattleConfig.WEAPON_TIER_BASE_MIGHT`, `WEAPON_TYPE_MIGHT_OFFSET`, `WEAPON_TIER_GRADE`).

| Tier (grade) | Sword | Axe | Spear | Bow | Dagger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wooden (E) | 0 / 90 / 4 | 0 / 75 / 8 | 1 / 85 / 6 | 0 / 100 / 3 | 0 / 95 / 1 |
| Bronze (D) | 3 / 90 / 5 | 3 / 75 / 10 | 4 / 85 / 7 | 2 / 100 / 4 | 1 / 95 / 2 |
| Iron (C) | 6 / 90 / 6 | 7 / 75 / 11 | 7 / 85 / 8 | 5 / 100 / 5 | 4 / 95 / 2 |
| Steel (B) | 9 / 85 / 8 | 10 / 70 / 13 | 10 / 80 / 10 | 8 / 95 / 7 | 7 / 90 / 3 |
| Silver (A) | 12 / 90 / 7 | 14 / 75 / 12 | 13 / 85 / 9 | 11 / 100 / 6 | 10 / 95 / 3 |
| Platinum (S) | 15 / 95 / 7 | 18 / 80 / 12 | 16 / 90 / 9 | 14 / 100 / 6 | 13 / 100 / 3 |

Mt / Hit / weight. Axes are the least accurate and heaviest, daggers the lightest, bows and daggers the most accurate; wooden is the lightest tier. Every bow is effective against Flying, every spear against Large.

- **Damage**: attack power + Mt (+ an art's bonus) - the target's DEF, at least 1. Attack power depends on the weapon type: sword and spear STR; axe STR + BLD / 2; bow (STR + DEX) / 2; dagger (STR + SPD) / 2; every half rounded down, from the stats as they stand in battle (after the weight penalty and any effects). A magic weapon uses MAG in place of STR (a magic bow is (MAG + DEX) / 2) and strikes against RES. Enemies (STR or MAG + their action's Mt) and spells (MAG + the spell's Mt) are not affected by weapon type. The rule is `CombatMath.attack_power`; between battles `RunCompanion.attack_power(w)` gives it from the stats a companion carries, and `RunCompanion.attack(w)` adds the weapon's Mt (the "Attack" figure: what a strike deals before DEF).
- **The magic weapons** are four of the rare weapons (see Rare weapons): Emberglass Sword Mt 5 and Whisper Fang (dagger) Mt 3 need E+; Moonthread Bow Mt 4 and Stormcall Pike Mt 6 need D. Each is 2 Mt above the bronze weapon of its type and strikes with MAG against RES; 150 gold at the Smithy. The hermit sells the Emberglass Sword.
- **The Smithy** rolls its stock on the first day of each week: 5 different weapons, each with a tier drawn by the week (`RunConfig.SHOP_TIER_WEIGHTS`): week 1 wooden and bronze; week 2 adds iron; week 3 steel; week 4 a little silver; weeks 5-6 mostly steel, some silver. Each slot is a two-proficiency weapon of that tier with a 20% chance when the tier has them (bronze to silver; `RunConfig.SHOP_HYBRID_CHANCE`), otherwise a ladder weapon of a random type (a slot that rolls a weapon already in stock is drawn again, so about 23% of bronze-silver slots end up two-proficiency). It never sells platinum. Prices by tier: 20, 50, 110, 200, 320, 480 gold; two-proficiency weapons 65, 140, 250, 400 (`Run.weapon_price`). On top of the five, each week has a 35% chance of one rare weapon (see Rare weapons), listed first; then by tier and type, a two-proficiency weapon after the ladder weapon of its type.
- **Events** that grant a weapon either give a set one or draw one when the event comes up: a tier drawn as the week's stock is, or (one time in four) one tier above the best the Smithy sells that week; then a two-proficiency weapon of that tier (and type) with the same 20% chance; and with a 15% chance a rare weapon of that type whose grade fits the week instead (`RunConfig.EVENT_RARE_CHANCE`). A Wounded Knight leaves a bow; the Ruined Armory any type.
- **Bosses**: beating the day 14 or day 28 boss drops one random weapon (any type) a tier above the best the Smithy sells that week: steel on day 14, platinum on day 28 (`Run.boss_weapon_tier`); on day 14 a two-proficiency steel weapon with the same 20% chance (there is no platinum one). With a 50% chance (`RunConfig.BOSS_RARE_CHANCE`) the boss drops a rare weapon in its place: on day 14 one needing grade D or less, on day 28 any. It goes to the armory and the battle's report names it (`BattleReport.weapon_found`). The day 42 boss ends the run and drops nothing.

### Rare weapons

Off the metal ladder: a rare weapon needs only a grade in its type (not a tier) and has one property no ladder weapon has. There are fourteen (`RunContent.rare_weapons`; `WeaponData.rare`, and `WeaponData.trait_lines()` writes the property for display): the four magic weapons above and these ten.

| Weapon | Type, grade | Mt / Hit / weight | Property | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oathkeeper | sword, D | grows / 90 / 5 | Mt is the ladder base Mt of the tier the wielder's sword grade can hold: 3 at D, 6 at C, 9 at B, 12 at A, 15 at S | 300 |
| Twinfang Sabre | sword, C | 3 / 80 / 6 | a plain Attack strikes twice, each rolled on its own (arts keep their own count) | 280 |
| Headsman | axe, C | 10 / 60 / 13 | Crit +25, Attack and arts | 280 |
| Bloodletter | axe, D | 5 / 70 / 9 | heals the wielder for half the damage it deals (rounded down, never above max HP) | 180 |
| Wallbreaker | spear, C | 5 / 75 / 10 | ignores half the target's DEF; with an art that ignores DEF too, the larger counts | 280 |
| Sentinel's Pike | spear, D | 5 / 80 / 9 | DEF +3 while held, and enemies are 1.5x as likely to aim at the wielder | 180 |
| Galewing Bow | bow, D | 3 / 90 / 3 | SPD +3 while held | 180 |
| Longwatch | bow, C | 7 / 110 / 7 | Crit +15 | 280 |
| Viper's Kiss | dagger, D | 2 / 95 / 2 | every hit that lands poisons: 2 damage a turn for 3 turns (a kill by it is the wielder's) | 180 |
| Magpie | dagger, C | 4 / 90 / 3 | +10 gold for every enemy its wielder fells in a won battle, in the battle's gold | 280 |

Spears are effective against Large and bows against Flying, as every spear and bow is. The properties are data on `WeaponData` (`might_by_grade`, `attack_strikes`, `crit_bonus`, `lifesteal_percent`, `ignore_def_percent`, `held_stats`, `target_weight_mult`, `on_hit_effect`, `gold_per_kill`), so another rare weapon is a new `.tres` in that list. The Attack figure and every forecast show the true numbers (Oathkeeper's Mt for its holder, two strikes for Twinfang).

Where they come from: each weekly Smithy restock has a 35% chance (`RunConfig.SHOP_RARE_CHANCE`) of one rare weapon, drawn from those whose grade fits the week (`RunConfig.RARE_GRADE_BY_WEEK`: none in week 1, grade D or less in week 2, any from week 3); the day 14 and day 28 bosses (50%) and events that give a weapon (15%), as under Weapons.

On screen a rare weapon carries a small red diamond before its name and the word "rare" before its type, in the Smithy's list and in the Barracks' armory, and each of its properties is written on a line of its own under the usual small print. In the Barracks Mt is what it would be in the chosen companion's hand (Oathkeeper's follows their grade; the Smithy writes "Mt by grade"). In battle the forecast of a plain Attack with a weapon that strikes twice reads "DMG 9x2" as an art's does, the sheet shown while Shift is held lists the held weapon's properties and, lower down, each of the companion's traits with what it does, and pointing at the weapon's line under the companion's name does too. After a won battle the card of whoever holds Magpie says in its hover text what the weapon brought in.

### Two-proficiency weapons

Six families need a grade in two types. Each **is its main type** (the first named) for everything: the attack power formula, the proficiency gained by using it, which arts can be used with it, effectiveness. The second type is only a requirement: using the weapon never raises it, so it rises only by training (see Training ground; a drill can open a weapon type or school from E) or by fighting with a weapon of that type, or comes from the companion's starting grades.

| Family | Main + second |
|---|---|
| Halberd | axe + spear |
| Glaive | spear + sword (effective against Large) |
| Greatsword | sword + axe |
| Twinblades | dagger + sword |
| Javelin | spear + bow (effective against Large) |
| Runeblade | sword + black magic: a magic weapon (MAG in place of STR, against RES) |

Each family exists at bronze, iron, steel and silver ("Bronze Halberd" ... "Silver Halberd"; `data/weapons/<tier>_<family>.tres`, listed in `RunContent.hybrid_weapons`, 24 in all). To hold one: the tier's grade in the main type and one grade lower in the second, but never below E+ (everyone has E in everything, so E would be no requirement; `BattleConfig.HYBRID_SECOND_GRADE_MIN`): bronze D + E+, iron C + D, steel B + C, silver A + B. Mt is that of the main type's ladder weapon one tier up (`BattleConfig.HYBRID_MIGHT_TIERS_UP`), weight 2 above the main type's weapon of the same tier (`HYBRID_WEIGHT_BONUS`), Hit as it; the price is the tier's x1.25, rounded to 5 (`RunConfig.HYBRID_PRICE_MULT`). Effective only per the main type: Glaive and Javelin against Large, the others against nothing.

| Tier (needs) | Halberd | Glaive | Greatsword | Twinblades | Javelin | Runeblade | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze (D + E+) | 7 / 75 / 12 | 7 / 85 / 9 | 6 / 90 / 7 | 4 / 95 / 4 | 7 / 85 / 9 | 6 / 90 / 7 | 65 |
| Iron (C + D) | 10 / 75 / 13 | 10 / 85 / 10 | 9 / 90 / 8 | 7 / 95 / 4 | 10 / 85 / 10 | 9 / 90 / 8 | 140 |
| Steel (B + C) | 14 / 70 / 15 | 13 / 80 / 12 | 12 / 85 / 10 | 10 / 90 / 5 | 13 / 80 / 12 | 12 / 85 / 10 | 250 |
| Silver (A + B) | 18 / 75 / 14 | 16 / 85 / 11 | 15 / 90 / 9 | 13 / 95 / 5 | 16 / 85 / 11 | 15 / 90 / 9 | 400 |

Mt / Hit / weight. `WeaponData.second_type` (a weapon type) or `second_school` (a magic school), with `second_grade`, hold the second requirement (-1 / unset for ordinary weapons); `WeaponData.requirement_text()` writes it for display ("axe C · spear D"; an ordinary weapon "spear C"). Every check of whether someone can hold a weapon goes through `CompanionProgress.weapon_block_reason(w)` (`can_equip_weapon` is it being empty), which names the first grade missing ("Needs spear D; Elena is E.").

## Combat arts

An art belongs to one weapon type, can only be used with a weapon of that type in hand, and costs stamina and a recovery like any action (always one turn). Tier 1: 4-6 STA, sits out 1 action; tier 2: 8-10 STA, 2 actions; tier 3: 13-15 STA, 3 actions. Every weapon type has 12 arts, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/arts/`, all listed in `art_library.tres`). A companion's signature art is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other user of that weapon can learn it. Effects are limited to bonus Mt/Hit/Crit, striking every enemy, striking twice, ignoring part of DEF, effectiveness against a trait, a debuff on each enemy hit (lowered stats, some of which stack; taking more damage; bleeding or poison every turn, which can kill) and a buff on the user (raised stats; taking less damage; being aimed at more or less often; a stance that answers physical blows: an enemy whose physical action lands on the user is hurt for a share of the damage, at least 1, and can die of it; magic is never answered). `WeaponData.unique_art` is reserved for rare weapons that grant an art of their own; nothing uses it yet, nor `CombatArtData.durability_cost`.

## Not built yet

Room is left for, but nothing exists of: weapon durability (`WeaponData.max_uses`, `CombatArtData.durability_cost`), spell or skill books, crafting, jobs for companions in reserve, skill books, and anything carried between runs.
