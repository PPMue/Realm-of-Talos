# Realm of Talos

2D roguelite party builder (Godot 4.7, GDScript). You command the last city and defend it from monsters for 30 days. Currently: a playable run (a calendar of battle, free, event and boss days) with countdown battles, weapon proficiency, combat arts and magic that carry through the run.

## Run

Open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or from a shell:

```
godot --path .
```

### A run

- The game opens on a title screen (begin a new run, window or full screen, leave). A run opens with a letter to the commander naming the two companions who answered the call.
- The city is the hub, and it is a place: its buildings are the buttons. The **Gate** is where the day's business is (march out on battle and boss days, hear what waits on event days, end a free day); the **Tavern** hires, the **Smithy** sells weapons, the **Inn** rests the company, the **Mason's Yard** mends the walls, the **Barracks** holds the party (always open), and the **Watchtower** reports the days ahead (always open). Buildings that cannot be used today are shuttered and say why. The building to go to next has a glowing name board.
- The sky is the clock. A free day runs from morning through afternoon to dusk as its two actions are spent; a battle day opens at dawn with the enemy's fires beyond the wall; a boss day has a red sky. A curtain with the day's number falls between days.
- A thin strip at the top shows the day, the next 7 days as medallions (swords = battle, sun = free day, letter = event, horned skull = boss; hover for details), the walls (city health, 100; at 0 the run is lost), gold, iron ore and timber. The company stands along the bottom as round portraits with their health as an arc; click one to open the barracks on them. A slip of parchment at the bottom left gives the day's news.
- Rules are not written on the screens: they are in tooltips, on the letters that buildings and events show before you commit, and in this file. Keys still work but are not drawn on buttons; a button's tooltip names its key. In the city: P barracks, G gate, W watchtower, Esc puts a letter down.
- 30 days. A boss on days 10, 20 and 30; the day before each boss is always free; day 1 is always a battle. Each block of ten has 5-6 battles, 2 free days and 1-2 events.
- You start with 2 random companions of the fourteen (ten fighters, two for each weapon, and four mages, two for each school; at least one of the two is never a mage). The two who share a weapon are built differently: Elena is all health and strength where Asterion is armour, Aina is balanced where Kaito is strength and crits with no defence, Erik is accurate where Koharu is swift and lucky, Ingrid is sturdy where Raizen is fast, Yuzu is speed and crits where Shiro is tough, Aveline heals where Elio protects, and Freya is a glass cannon where Sylas is slow, hardy and hinders. Up to 4 are fielded, left to right (rightmost = nearest the enemies); the rest wait in reserve. In the Barracks the company stands in its line behind the muster rail, rear to front, with the reserve behind the bench's board below (the art is cut out, so they stand free against the wall and run over into each other's places): carry someone to another place or to the bench to move them (someone from the bench set down on a full line changes places with whoever stood there), or use the orders on their page (keys: left and right arrows, F, A). The page on the right is the muster roll for whoever is selected: their stats, each with a line under it as long as its growth rate (the chance it rises by 1 on a level-up, with the figure beside it), what they hold, and the armory to take a weapon from (a weapon not allowed says why: the grade it needs). It also opens the combat arts screen. All of this is free on any day until you commit to the day's activity.
- Levels: every companion starts at level 1. A won battle grants an XP pool (by danger, growing with the day; shown on each front) split evenly among the fielded companions still in the run at its end, injured ones included; reserve companions and lost battles give none. On each level-up every stat but BLD rolls its own growth rate (each companion's own, about 40% on average) to rise by 1, and at least 2 stats always rise. Max HP gained raises current HP too. Tavern hires join at the roster's average level, rounded down. A companion fielded in every battle in a party of four ends the run around level 13.
- HP carries over between days. At the end of every day each companion heals 20% of max HP. Stamina is full at the start of every battle. A companion injured in battle comes back at 1 HP, won or lost; one removed in battle is gone for the rest of the run (their weapon returns to the armory).
- **Battle day**: the Gate opens the war table with 2 or 3 fronts (keys 1-3), each with its enemies, a danger of 1-3, the reward and the city health lost if the battle is lost (15/20/25). Fronts you do not pick have no consequence. Enemies get stronger as the days pass.
- **Boss day**: one front. Losing ends the run; beating the day 30 boss wins it. Each boss day draws one of three bosses when the run begins (see Bosses). The day strip marks the boss's day with a horned skull, and when the next boss day is beyond the days shown, one more medallion after a gap marks it; hover a boss medallion, or climb the Watchtower, for every boss still ahead with its one-line warning. The boss card shows the boss large, its name, what makes it dangerous and any escorts.
- **Free day**: 2 actions, each once a day: rest at the Inn (everyone to full HP and every spell back to full uses; nothing else restores spell uses), buy at the Smithy (weapons; any number of purchases is one action; rare magic weapons turn up now and then from day 8), hire at the Tavern (1 of 1-2 companions offered, mages included), mend the walls at the Mason's Yard (materials for city health). End the day at the Gate when done; unused actions are lost.
- **Event day**: a letter with a short story and 2-3 choices (keys 1-3; it can be put down and picked up again at the Gate) that trade gold, materials, city health, companions' HP or a free weapon.
- The run is lost when the city reaches 0, a boss wins, or no companions are left.

### Battles

- The company is the row of portraits along the bottom; the rightmost stands nearest the enemies and is aimed at most. The small roundel on each portrait is how often the enemy aims at that place. Someone who has just acted is dimmed under an hourglass with the number of actions until they are ready again, the same picture an enemy's countdown uses; someone injured is reddened and sunk. Click anyone who is ready to choose who acts: they step up out of the line, and their portrait fills the left edge of the screen.
- Give an order from the list beside them, or press its number: Attack, the companion's equipped combat arts (up to 3), Magic (for anyone who knows a spell), Guard, Focus. Point at an order to read what it does under the list; an art you cannot afford is greyed out and says why.
- Magic changes the list to the companion's spells, each with its school, tier and uses left (number keys; Back, Esc or right-click returns; more than six spells page with More). A spell with no uses left is greyed out and says why.
- For Attack and single-target arts, point at an enemy to see hit %, damage and crit %, then click it to strike. Arts that strike every enemy light up every enemy at once; click any of them to confirm. Right-click or Esc backs out.
- A spell aimed at a companion is cast by clicking their portrait below; each portrait shows what it would do (for a heal, HP before and after). A spell for every ally or every enemy lights them all up; one click confirms.
- Under each enemy: its HP, an hourglass with the turns left until it acts, and the kind of action coming. Every action you take, art, spell or not, is one turn (the code and the data files call a turn a `tick`). A pending action already pushed back by a spell shows its countdown in pale blue with a DELAYED tag; one brought forward by another enemy's order shows it in orange with a HASTENED tag (and pops SOONER -N).
- Traits such as FLYING show as a tag above an enemy's name (hover it). The forecast says EFFECTIVE when the strike doubles Mt, and MAGIC when it is MAG against RES.
- Lasting effects show on the unit with the turns left (on party panels under the status tag; poison and burning in green-yellow). Poison pops its damage at the end of each turn.
- A reflecting stance wraps the enemy in a ring of pink thorns with a REFLECTS N% tag; the forecast turns pink and says what striking costs you (normal and critical). Spells pass through.
- An enemy that aims from the left carries an AIMS LEFT tag. The party panels' "targeted N%" then counts it: with only such enemies the figure is the mirrored one ("aims from the left"); with both kinds the panel shows both ("targeted N% · from the left N%").
- A second phase gets the big banner (PHASE TWO, its name and one line) and a tag under the enemy. Recovery added by an enemy pops +N RECOVERY on the panel; an enemy's heal pops +N on it.
- When nobody is ready, Wait (1 or Space) lets a turn pass. L opens the battle log. Clicking during playback speeds it up.
- Hold Shift while pointing at an enemy or a companion for what is known about them: stats, grades, arts, traits and what is on them now. Let go of Shift, or move off them, and the sheet goes. Nothing of the kind pops up on hover alone.

Proficiency and arts:

- Each companion has a grade in every weapon type: E, E+, D, D+, C, C+, B, B+, A, A+, S. Attacking gives 1 point in the type of the weapon held, a combat art 2; Guard, Focus and Wait give none. One companion can gain at most 8 points per battle. The grade and progress for the weapon held show under the companion's name on the left.
- Every grade reached teaches one random art of that weapon type the companion does not know yet (higher grades roll higher tiers), equipped straight away if a slot is free. A banner announces it.
- Weapons need a grade to be held: iron any grade, steel C, silver B (silver is sold from day 11).
- After a battle the result comes in three beats, and a click, Space or Enter moves it on. First: won or lost, what it gave (gold, materials, XP) or cost the walls, and the company in a row of cards with their XP bars filling; a card shows the level, weapon and magic grades with what the battle added, anything learned, and INJURED or LOST across the face (hover a card for the exact numbers). Then every level gained is shown on its own: the companion stands beside their ten stats and a light goes down the list one stat at a time; where it stops, the number goes up. The gains were already rolled when the XP was granted; the screen only reveals them in order. Last, the cards again and the way back to the city.

### Magic

- Two schools: **Black Magic** (offence) and **White Magic** (support). Every companion has a grade in each, with the same grades and thresholds as weapons. Casting gives 4 points in the spell's school, up to 12 magic points per battle (separate from the weapon cap). Every school grade reached teaches a random spell of that school not yet known, with the same tier odds, no-duplicate rule and fall-through as combat arts. All known spells can be cast; there are no slots.
- A spell costs one turn and no stamina, then a recovery of its tier (1-3). Each spell has a number of uses per companion (6 / 4 / 2 by tier, set per spell); uses last between battles and days and only Rest restores them.
- Black spells: damage = caster MAG + spell Mt - target RES (at least 1), with the normal accuracy and crit formulas using the spell's own Hit. They may strike every enemy, ignore part of RES, debuff the target, and delay the target's pending action: a pending action can be delayed only once; the next one it picks can be delayed again. Combat arts never touch countdowns.
- White spells always succeed: heals (caster MAG + the spell's power, on anyone still in the line; lifting an injured companion above 0 HP clears the injury; companions removed from the run cannot be healed), buffs, less damage taken, a veil (enemies aim elsewhere) or a beacon (enemies aim here), and removing debuffs, on one ally or all. Healing spells can also be cast from a mage's page in the Barracks between battles, spending uses.
- Enemy actions are physical (STR against DEF) or magic (MAG against RES); Guard halves both. The Goblin Wizard, the casters of the bestiary, the Rimeclaw Wyvern's frost, the Oathbreaker's Black Tide, and the Faceless Princess, Forsaken Seraph and Eclipse Sovereign are magic. Enemy MAG grows with the day like STR.
- **Magic weapons** are rare variants of the normal types: Attack and arts with them use MAG against RES with the weapon's own Mt.
- **Effectiveness**: enemies carry traits (so far Flying). Weapons, arts and spells list the traits they are effective against; every bow is effective against Flying. An effective strike doubles Mt (the weapon's, or the spell's) before DEF or RES is subtracted; effective through both weapon and art still doubles once. The multiplier is `BattleConfig.EFFECTIVE_MIGHT_MULT`.
- The arts screen (Arts and Magic) shows both magic grades and every known spell with uses left; the Barracks shows spell uses and casts heals; the battle result lists magic grades gained and spells learned.

## Bestiary

Every normal enemy belongs to a band (`EnemyData.band`); its groups never appear before the band begins, and fronts favour the band of the day (`RunConfig.BAND_AGE_WEIGHT`: older groups still turn up, scaled to the day). Danger 3 fronts from the middle band on can field four enemies.

- **Early (days 1-9)**: Orc, Goblin Thief, Goblin Wizard, Stalker, Skeleton Bird*, Winged Zombie*, Slime Monster (soaks blows, re-forms, slows), Briarwing* (thorn mantle reflects 50%).
- **Middle (days 11-19)**: Cursed Warrior, Armored Beast, Minotaur, Blackwing* (fast diver), Poisoned Wings* (poisons the line), Flame Eagle* (fire caster, burning), Poison Spawn (heavy poison, corrodes DEF/RES), One-Eye* (debuffer, its gaze adds recovery), Ancient Treant (protector: buffs and heals every enemy).
- **Late (days 21-29)**: Stormcrest* (lightning caster), Void Raven* (its caw orders the others to act sooner), Shadowed Wings* (assassin that aims from the left), Faceless Spawn (burner), Cursed Dweller (slow heavy hitter, stacking frenzy), Forgotten Guardian* (wards every enemy, frost aegis reflects).

\* Flying. Each enemy's data is in `data/enemies/`, its groups in `data/run/groups/`.

## Bosses

`data/enemies/bosses/` and `data/run/bosses/` (a boss group: the boss, then any escorts, who grow with the day; `description` is the one-line warning). Each boss day draws one from its pool by the run's seed (`Run.boss_draw`). Every boss has one idea:

- **Day 10**: The Horned Tyrant (its War Bellow stacks: it grows stronger the longer the fight runs), Mossback Troll (regenerates 18% of its HP on every Buff it shows: burst it down), Rampart Golem (high DEF, no RES, slow and crushing, braces to halve damage: magic, DEF-ignoring arts and Guard).
- **Day 20**: Rimeclaw Wyvern (flies, frost magic, Take Wing makes it very hard to hit: bows and accuracy), The Oathbreaker (its Oath of Thorns, a Defend, reflects 80% of weapon and art damage: read the intent, use spells), The Fallen Commander (with a One-Eye and a Flame Eagle: rallies every enemy and orders them to act sooner; alone it makes a last stand: choose the kill order).
- **Day 30**: The Faceless Princess (aims from the left and poisons: put the sturdiest on the left), The Forsaken Seraph (flies, magic, charges Judgement on everyone: RES, bows, speed and a delay spell), The Eclipse Sovereign (adds recovery to everyone; at half HP turns faster and harsher).

### Enemy mechanics

All in the battle rules and driven by data (`EnemyActionData`, `EnemyData`, `StatusEffectData`):
- **Damage over time** (`damage_per_tick`): at the end of every turn; Guard does not halve it. It can injure a companion but never removes an injured one. White magic that removes debuffs clears it.
- **Reflection** (`reflect_percent`): a weapon strike or combat art that lands on the bearer deals that share of its damage (at least 1) back to the striker. Spells are exempt.
- **Healing** (`heal_percent`) on the enemy itself, or on every enemy with the ALL_ALLIES targeting (which also spreads buffs).
- **Reversed targeting** (`EnemyData.reversed_targeting` or per action): the position weights mirrored.
- **Orders** (`hasten_allies`): every other enemy's countdown drops by N, never below 1.
- **Recovery pressure** (`add_recovery`): adds to the recovery of each companion reached. A lone companion still ignores recovery.
- **Phases** (`phase_trigger`: at an HP share, or when the last one standing): a second action list, a banner, an optional lasting effect, and the pending action is replaced.
- Also: stacking effects (`max_stacks`) and actions never picked twice in a row (`no_repeat`).

## Tests

```
godot --headless --path . --import          # first time / after adding scripts: builds the class cache
godot --headless --path . -s res://tests/run_tests.gd
```

Exit code 0 means every test passed. `TEST_ONLY=playthrough` runs only tests whose name contains that text (several, comma-separated), `TEST_SKIP=playthrough` skips them; `TEST_VERBOSE=1` prints each test's name. Besides the battle rules (`run_tests.gd`), the run rules (`run_rule_tests.gd`), magic and effectiveness (`magic_tests.gd`) and the bestiary, bosses and their mechanics (`bestiary_tests.gd`), the run prints a random-policy battle win rate, how fast grades rise, and an automated playthrough: a simple policy (`run_policy.gd`) plays 200 whole runs through the rule classes and reports how often it wins, on which day runs end, and for each boss how often it was faced and beaten. The policy answers the mechanics in obvious ways: it never strikes into a reflecting stance, delays a long charged attack on everyone, guards a heavy blow aimed at it, and stands its sturdiest on the left against a boss that aims from the left.

## Layout

- `scripts/battle/` battle rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, seeded `RandomNumberGenerator` injected. `battle_config.gd` holds every tuning number (grade thresholds, points per action, the per-battle cap, the tier odds per grade). `proficiency.gd` is the grade rules (including whether a companion may equip a weapon), `art_learning.gd` picks the art learned on a grade-up.
- `scripts/run/` the run rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, every random choice from an injected seeded generator. `Run` (the day flow, city, gold and materials, roster and line, today's pending choice, the outcome; commands return false or a reason when not allowed), `RunCalendar`, `RunCompanion` (data, progress, HP between days), `Levels` (XP per level and the growth rolls), `RunInventory`/`OwnedWeapon` (the armory: each weapon held by at most one companion), `Front`, `BattleReport`, and `run_config.gd` with every run tuning number (day counts, rewards, city damage, prices, enemy scaling). `CompanionProgress` is what a companion carries between battles: level, XP and stat gains, proficiency, known and equipped arts, and the weapon in hand; `Battle` reads and updates it.
- `scripts/data/` `Resource` classes for companions, enemies, weapons, combat arts, the art library, spells (`SpellData`) and the spell library, enemy traits (`EnemyTraits`), enemy actions and effects. `scripts/battle/spell_learning.gd` picks the spell learned on a school grade-up; `CombatMath.DamageType` is physical or magic.
- `data/` the `.tres` data: companions (stats, growth rates, weapon, starting grades, signature art), enemies (with their action lists; bosses in `enemies/bosses/`), weapons (iron, steel, silver of each type), `arts/<weapon type>/` combat arts, `arts/art_library.tres` (every art that can be learned; signature arts are not in it), `spells/black/` and `spells/white/` spells with `spells/spell_library.tres` (every spell that can be learned; the mages' signature spells are not in it), `battles/prototype_battle.tres` (used by the battle tests), and `run/`: `run_content.tres` lists everything a run is built from: the companions, front `sites/`, enemy `groups/` (danger and the days they appear), `bosses/` (one per boss day), `events/` and `shop/` stock. A new event, group or shop item is a new `.tres` added to that list; no code.
- `scripts/ui/`, `scenes/main.tscn` (the main scene). `game.gd` shows the title (`title_screen.gd`) and then the city. `city_screen.gd` is the hub: it reads the run, sets each building's state, and opens the war table (`war_table.gd`), the party (`party_screen.gd`, which opens `arts_screen.gd`), the smithy and tavern (`store_screen.gd`), letters (`letter_panel.gd`: visits, events, the watch's report, the opening), the battle (`battle_screen.gd`) and the run-end screen over itself. The battle screen plays a battle the run prepared (`Run.start_battle`), emits `finished`, and shows the `BattleReport` the run hands back. Screens only read run and battle state and call their commands.
- The look: things from the world rather than an interface. `ui_theme.gd` holds the colours, the fonts (a book serif; system fonts for now) and the shared drawing (framed boards with cut corners and grain, parchment sheets). `SlantPanel`, `SlantBar`, `ActionButton` and `RowButton` are the shared pieces (the names date from when panels leaned; `UiTheme.SLANT` is 0 now); the two buttons have a `paper` style for lines written on parchment. In battle, `action_menu.gd` is the close-up and the orders (light on a soft-edged veil of shadow, so the art and the field show through; `ActionButton`'s `veil` style) and `party_panel.gd` one portrait of the line; in the Barracks, `muster_figure.gd` is one standing figure, which can be carried and set down. Companion art (`assets/character/`) is a cut-out with no background: wherever a face is shown in a frame or a ring the UI paints a plain ground behind it, and `CompanionData.figure_scale` evens out how large each was painted so heads match side by side (`portrait_focus` says where the face is). `ui_icons.gd` draws the small pictures. After a battle, `battle_result.gd` runs the result (`spoils_card.gd` is one companion's card, `level_up_panel.gd` the stat-by-stat reveal of one level).
- The city is drawn in code, with no image files: `city_art.gd` is the kit (walls, roofs, windows, doors, flames), `city_building.gd` the seven buildings, `city_backdrop.gd` the scene and its times of day (`set_phase`), `interior_backdrop.gd` the inside of a building, `stage_backdrop.gd` the battlefield. Where each building stands is the `RECTS` table in `city_building.gd`. Painted art can replace any of these later without touching the screens.
- The layout is designed at 1920x1080 and scales with the window.
- `tests/` headless tests: `run_tests.gd` (battle rules, and the runner), `run_rule_tests.gd` (run rules and the playthrough), `run_policy.gd` (the automated player).
- `assets/` character and enemy art.

## Combat arts

An art belongs to one weapon type, can only be used with a weapon of that type in hand, and costs stamina and a recovery like any action (always one turn). Tier 1: 4-6 STA, sits out 1 action; tier 2: 8-10 STA, 2 actions; tier 3: 13-15 STA, 3 actions. Every weapon type has 12 arts, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/arts/`, all listed in `art_library.tres`). A companion's signature art is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other user of that weapon can learn it. Effects are limited to bonus Mt/Hit/Crit, striking every enemy, striking twice, ignoring part of DEF, effectiveness against a trait, a debuff on each enemy hit (lowered stats, some of which stack; taking more damage; bleeding or poison every turn, which can kill) and a buff on the user (raised stats; taking less damage; being aimed at more or less often; a stance that answers physical blows: an enemy whose physical action lands on the user is hurt for a share of the damage, at least 1, and can die of it; magic is never answered). `WeaponData.unique_art` is reserved for rare weapons that grant an art of their own; nothing uses it yet, nor `CombatArtData.durability_cost`.

## Not built yet

Room is left for, but nothing exists of: weapon durability (`WeaponData.max_uses`, `CombatArtData.durability_cost`), wooden weapons, spell or skill books, crafting and the smithy (iron ore is only traded and used for repairs so far), city buildings, jobs for companions in reserve, skill books, and anything carried between runs.
