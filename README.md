# Realm of Talos

2D roguelite party builder (Godot 4.7, GDScript). You command the last city and defend it from monsters for 42 days, six weeks. Currently: a playable run (a calendar of battle, free and boss days, with events at the start of some free days) with countdown battles, weapon proficiency, combat arts and magic that carry through the run.

## Run

Open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or from a shell:

```
godot --path .
```

### A run

- The game opens on a title screen (begin a new run, window or full screen, leave). A run opens with a letter to the commander naming the two companions who answered the call.
- The city is the hub, and it is a place: its buildings are the buttons. The **Gate** is where the day's business is (march out on battle and boss days, hear the matter that opens some free days, end a free day); the **Tavern** hires, the **Smithy** sells weapons, the **Inn** rests the company, the **Mason's Yard** mends the walls, the **Barracks** holds the party (always open), and the **Watchtower** reports the days ahead (always open). Buildings that cannot be used today are shuttered and say why. The building to go to next has a glowing name board.
- The sky is the clock. A free day runs from morning to dusk once its action is spent; a battle day opens at dawn with the enemy's fires beyond the wall; a boss day has a red sky. A curtain with the day's number falls between days.
- A thin strip at the top shows the day, the next 7 days as medallions (swords = battle, sun = free day, letter = a free day that begins with an event, horned skull = boss; hover for details), the walls (city health, 100; at 0 the run is lost), gold, iron ore and timber. The company stands along the bottom as round portraits with their health as an arc; click one to open the barracks on them. A slip of parchment at the bottom left gives the day's news.
- Rules are not written on the screens: they are in tooltips, on the letters that buildings and events show before you commit, and in this file. Keys still work but are not drawn on buttons; a button's tooltip names its key. In the city: P barracks, G gate, W watchtower, Esc puts a letter down.
- 42 days in six weeks of seven. Every week has the same shape: battle, free, battle, free, battle, free, and then a seventh day that is a free day in weeks 1, 3 and 5 and a boss in weeks 2, 4 and 6 (days 14, 28 and 42). So battles and free days alternate, day 1 is a battle, and the day before every boss is free; the only breaks are the two free days that end weeks 1, 3 and 5 and the battle that follows each boss. A run has 18 battles, 3 bosses and 21 free days (`RunCalendar`).
- Events: one free day in every week begins with an event (six a run), never the day before a boss. The days are drawn when the run begins and show on the strip. An event does not repeat while unseen ones remain.
- The week: the Tavern's offers, the Smithy's stock and the training ground's drills are rolled on the first day of each week (days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36) and stay the same all week. Someone hired or a weapon bought is gone until the next week; a companion lost in battle is never offered again. What the Smithy sells climbs with the weeks (see Weapons).
- You start with 2 random companions of the fourteen (ten fighters, two for each weapon, and four mages, two for each school; at least one of the two is never a mage). The two who share a weapon are built differently: Elena is all health and strength where Asterion is armour, Aina is balanced where Kaito is strength and crits with no defence, Erik is accurate where Koharu is swift and lucky, Ingrid is sturdy where Raizen is fast, Yuzu is speed and crits where Shiro is tough, Aveline heals where Elio protects, and Freya is a glass cannon where Sylas is slow, hardy and hinders. Everyone starts with 16-24 health (Elena the most, Elio the least) and 12-20 stamina (every fighter more than every mage), and with the wooden weapon of their type. Up to 4 are fielded, left to right (rightmost = nearest the enemies); the rest wait in reserve. In the Barracks the company stands in its line behind the muster rail, rear to front, with the reserve behind the bench's board below (the art is cut out, so they stand free against the wall; each stands in a bay of their own between timber posts, and nobody's art covers a neighbour): carry someone to another place or to the bench to move them (someone from the bench set down on a full line changes places with whoever stood there), or use the orders on their page (keys: left and right arrows, F, A). The page on the right is the muster roll for whoever is selected: their stats, each with a line under it as long as its growth rate (the chance it rises by 1 on a level-up, with the figure beside it), what they hold and their Attack with it (attack power for that kind of weapon plus its Mt: what a strike deals before the target's defence; each weapon they could take shows the Attack it would give), and the armory to take a weapon from (a weapon not allowed says why: the grade it needs). It also opens the combat arts screen. All of this is free on any day until you commit to the day's activity.
- Levels: every companion starts at level 1. A won battle grants an XP pool (by danger, growing with the day; shown on each front) shared among the fielded companions still in the run at its end, injured ones included; reserve companions, companions removed in the battle and lost battles get none. A quarter of the pool is split evenly, so someone who did little still gains a little; the other three quarters are split by the contribution points each earned in that battle: a landed Attack 2 (a miss 1), a combat art used 3, a spell cast 3 (of any kind), each enemy blow that lands on them 1 (2 while guarding), and 4 more for landing the killing blow on an enemy (an art that kills several earns it for each; a kill by bleeding, poison or blight is whoever applied it, a kill by a reflecting stance or ward is whoever carries it). Focus and Wait earn nothing. If nobody earned a point the whole pool is split evenly. Shares are rounded down and the XP left over goes 1 at a time to the highest scorers, so the whole pool is always handed out (`Levels.split_xp`; the points are `BattleConfig.POINTS_*`, the even share `RunConfig.XP_EVEN_SHARE_PERCENT`). On each level-up every stat but BLD rolls its own growth rate (each companion's own, about 40% on average) to rise by 1, and at least 2 stats always rise. Max HP gained raises current HP too. Tavern hires join at the roster's average level, rounded down. A companion fielded in every battle in a party of four who earns an average share ends the run around level 13.
- HP carries over between days. At the end of every day each companion heals 20% of max HP. Stamina is full at the start of every battle. A companion injured in battle comes back at 1 HP, won or lost; one removed in battle is gone for the rest of the run (their weapon returns to the armory). Healing spells are cast only in battle: between battles the company recovers by the daily heal and by Rest at the Inn.
- **Battle day**: the Gate opens the war table with 2 or 3 fronts (keys 1-3), each with its enemies, a danger of 1-3, the reward and the city health lost if the battle is lost (15/20/25). Fronts you do not pick have no consequence. Enemies get stronger as the days pass.
- **Boss day**: one front. Losing ends the run; beating the day 42 boss wins it. Each boss day draws one of three bosses when the run begins (see Bosses). The day strip marks the boss's day with a horned skull, and when the next boss day is beyond the days shown, one more medallion after a gap marks it; hover a boss medallion, or climb the Watchtower, for every boss still ahead with its one-line warning. The boss card shows the boss large, its name, what makes it dangerous and any escorts.
- **Free day**: 1 action, one of: rest at the Inn (everyone to full HP and every spell back to full uses; nothing else restores spell uses), buy at the Smithy (weapons from the week's stock, one of each; any number of purchases the same day is the one action; see Weapons for what it sells), hire at the Tavern (from the week's 1-2 companions offered, mages included; further hires the same day are free too), mend the walls at the Mason's Yard (materials for city health), train one companion at the training ground (see Training ground). End the day at the Gate when done; an unused action is lost.
- **Event**: on a free day that begins with one, a letter with a short story and 2-3 choices (keys 1-3; it can be put down and picked up again at the Gate) that trade gold, materials, city health, companions' HP or a free weapon (a set one, or one drawn when the event comes up with a tier that fits the week; the result names it). It comes first: the buildings stay shut and the day cannot end until it is answered; then the free day goes on with its action as usual. If an event brings the walls to 0, the city falls at once.
- The run is lost when the city reaches 0, a boss wins, or no companions are left.

### Training ground

A free-day action (`Run.FreeAction.TRAIN`, "Train"), spent and blocked like the others: one a day, not on battle or boss days, not before the day's event is answered.

- **The week's drills** (`Run.drills`), posted with the weekly restock: always **Sparring**, plus two proficiency drills, each a different weapon type or magic school drawn at random from those somebody in the roster could train (any weapon type somebody is below S in; a school only if somebody is above E and below S in it), so a posted drill is never useless to everyone. With fewer such types, fewer are posted (with none, only Sparring). Weapon drills are listed before school drills. A drill is not used up: the same one can be taken on every free day of the week.
- **Taking one**: pick a drill and a companion in the roster, fielded or on the bench. It costs the day's action and 15 gold (`RunConfig.DRILL_COST`). A weapon drill can be taken by anyone below S in its type, including someone at E with no points: that is how a new weapon type is opened (reaching E+ teaches an art like any grade). A school drill needs a grade above E in it already (training never opens a school) and not S; Sparring needs a level below 20. `Run.drill_block_reason(drill, companion)` says why not; `Run.train(drill, companion)` returns a `DrillResult`. If nobody could learn from any posted drill, the action itself is blocked.
- **Quality** is rolled: poor 25%, good 50%, great 25% (`RunConfig.DRILL_QUALITY_WEIGHTS`).
- **Proficiency drill**: poor 3, good 5, great 8 points toward the next grade (`RunConfig.DRILL_POINTS`), never past S, with no per-battle cap. Good is about one battle's worth: a companion gains 4.8 points a battle on average with their weapon (capped at 8). Every grade reached teaches a random art (weapon) or spell (school) exactly as a grade reached in battle does.
- **Sparring**: good = half an even share of the day's danger-2 battle XP pool: pool / 4 / 2 (on day 1: 86 / 8 = 11 XP; day 41: 23); poor = 60% of good, great = 150% (`RunConfig.DRILL_XP_PERCENT`), rounded. Level-ups are rolled as after a battle, and max HP gained raises HP.
- The result (`DrillResult`) carries the drill, the companion, the quality and its word, the gold paid, points gained, grade before and after with each grade reached (and the art or spell it taught), XP gained, and the level-ups in the `BattleReport.level_ups` shape plus the level each rose from and the stats before it.

### Battles

- The company is the row of portraits along the bottom; the rightmost stands nearest the enemies and is aimed at most. The small roundel on each portrait is how often the enemy aims at that place. Someone who has just acted is dimmed under an hourglass with the number of actions until they are ready again, the same picture an enemy's countdown uses; someone injured is reddened and sunk. Click anyone who is ready to choose who acts: they step up out of the line, and their portrait fills the left edge of the screen.
- Give an order from the list beside them, or press its number: Attack, the companion's equipped combat arts (up to 3), Magic (for anyone who knows a spell), Guard, Focus (restores 5 stamina, about a tier 1 art's cost). Point at an order to read what it does under the list; an art you cannot afford is greyed out and says why.
- Magic changes the list to the companion's spells, each with its school, tier and uses left (number keys; Back, Esc or right-click returns; more than six spells page with More). A spell with no uses left is greyed out and says why.
- For Attack and single-target arts, point at an enemy to see hit %, damage and crit %, then click it to strike. Arts that strike every enemy light up every enemy at once; click any of them to confirm. Right-click or Esc backs out.
- A spell aimed at a companion is cast by clicking their portrait below; each portrait shows what it would do (for a heal, HP before and after). A spell for every ally or every enemy lights them all up; one click confirms.
- Under each enemy: its HP, an hourglass with the turns left until it acts, and the kind of action coming. Every action you take, art, spell or not, is one turn (the code and the data files call a turn a `tick`). A pending action already pushed back by a spell shows its countdown in pale blue with a DELAYED tag; one brought forward by another enemy's order shows it in orange with a HASTENED tag (and pops SOONER -N).
- Traits such as FLYING show as a tag above an enemy's name (hover it). The forecast says EFFECTIVE when the strike deals half as much again, and MAGIC when it is against RES.
- Lasting effects show on the unit with the turns left (on party panels under the status tag; poison and burning in green-yellow). Poison pops its damage at the end of each turn.
- A reflecting stance wraps the enemy in a ring of pink thorns with a REFLECTS N% tag; the forecast turns pink and says what striking costs you (normal and critical). Spells pass through.
- An enemy that aims from the left carries an AIMS LEFT tag. The party panels' "targeted N%" then counts it: with only such enemies the figure is the mirrored one ("aims from the left"); with both kinds the panel shows both ("targeted N% · from the left N%").
- A second phase gets the big banner (PHASE TWO, its name and one line) and a tag under the enemy. Recovery added by an enemy pops +N RECOVERY on the panel; an enemy's heal pops +N on it.
- When nobody is ready, Wait (1 or Space) lets a turn pass. L opens the battle log. Clicking during playback speeds it up.
- Hold Shift while pointing at an enemy or a companion for what is known about them: stats, grades, arts, traits and what is on them now. Let go of Shift, or move off them, and the sheet goes. Nothing of the kind pops up on hover alone.

Proficiency and arts:

- Each companion has a grade in every weapon type: E, E+, D, D+, C, C+, B, B+, A, A+, S. Attacking gives 1 point in the type of the weapon held, a combat art 2; Guard, Focus and Wait give none. One companion can gain at most 8 points per battle. The grade and progress for the weapon held show under the companion's name on the left.
- Every grade reached teaches one random art of that weapon type the companion does not know yet (higher grades roll higher tiers), equipped straight away if a slot is free. A banner announces it.
- Weapons need a grade to be held: wooden E, bronze D, iron C, steel B, silver A, platinum S (see Weapons); two-proficiency weapons also need a grade one lower (at least E+) in a second type. Most companions start at D in their own weapon, so bronze is their first upgrade.
- After a battle the result comes in three beats, and a click, Space or Enter moves it on. First: won or lost, what it gave (gold, materials, XP) or cost the walls, and the company in a row of cards with their XP bars filling, each by their own share; a card shows the level, the XP they got, a skull on the face for every enemy they felled, gold marks beside the name of whoever earned the most contribution points (when one companion stands out), weapon and magic grades with what the battle added, anything learned, and INJURED or LOST across the face (hover a card for the exact numbers). Then every level gained is shown on its own: the companion stands beside their ten stats and a light goes down the list one stat at a time; where it stops, the number goes up. The gains were already rolled when the XP was granted; the screen only reveals them in order. Last, the cards again and the way back to the city.

### Magic

- Two schools: **Black Magic** (offence) and **White Magic** (support). Every companion has a grade in each, with the same grades and thresholds as weapons. Casting gives 4 points in the spell's school, up to 12 magic points per battle (separate from the weapon cap). Every school grade reached teaches a random spell of that school not yet known, with the same tier odds, no-duplicate rule and fall-through as combat arts. All known spells can be cast; there are no slots. Each school has 12 spells, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/spells/`, all listed in `spell_library.tres`). A mage's signature spell is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other mage of that school can learn it.
- A spell costs one turn and no stamina, then a recovery of its tier (1-3). Each spell has a number of uses per companion (6 / 4 / 2 by tier, set per spell); uses last between battles and days and only Rest restores them.
- Black spells: damage = caster MAG + spell Mt - target RES (at least 1), with the normal accuracy and crit formulas using the spell's own Hit. They may strike every enemy, ignore part of RES, debuff the target (lowered stats, taking more damage, or damage every turn, which can kill: Blight), and delay the target's pending action: a pending action can be delayed only once; the next one it picks can be delayed again. Combat arts never touch countdowns.
- White spells always succeed: heals (caster MAG + the spell's power, on anyone still in the line; lifting an injured companion above 0 HP clears the injury; companions removed from the run cannot be healed), buffs, less damage taken, a veil (enemies aim elsewhere) or a beacon (enemies aim here), a reflecting ward (Thornward: like an art's stance, an enemy whose physical action lands on the ally is hurt for a share of the damage and can die of it; magic is never answered), and removing debuffs, on one ally or all.
- Enemy actions are physical (STR against DEF) or magic (MAG against RES); Guard halves both. The Goblin Wizard, the casters of the bestiary, the Rimeclaw Wyvern's frost, the Oathbreaker's Black Tide, and the Faceless Princess, Forsaken Seraph and Eclipse Sovereign are magic. Enemy MAG grows with the day like STR.
- **Magic weapons** are rare variants of the normal types: Attack and arts with them use MAG in place of STR in their type's formula (see Weapons), against RES, with the weapon's own Mt.
- **Effectiveness**: enemies carry traits (Flying and Large). Weapons, arts and spells list the traits they are effective against; every bow is effective against Flying and every spear against Large. An effective strike or spell deals half as much damage again: 50% of the damage left after DEF or RES, rounded down, added before a critical doubles it and before Guard or other damage-taken multipliers. Effective through both weapon and art still counts once. The share is `BattleConfig.EFFECTIVE_DAMAGE_BONUS_PERCENT`.
- Arts and Magic, reached from the Barracks, is the drill book: the company listed down the left, the one chosen standing beside an open book. Its left page is their grade in every weapon and both schools, each with a line for the way to the next grade; its right page is every art they know (click one to carry it into battle or leave it behind; three are carried) and every known spell with uses left. What the art or spell under the pointer does is written at the foot of the page.
- The Drill Yard is a fenced ring of sand in the square. Inside, the company is listed down the left, the one chosen stands in the yard, and the sergeant's board on the right carries the week's three notices. Each notice shows where the chosen companion stands (their grade or level, with the line toward the next) and how far a poor, good or great drill would carry them, or why they cannot take it. The slip at the foot sets them to it. How it went is then written up on the board: a poor, good or great drill, the line filling by what it gave, the new grade and anything learned; a level gained from sparring is shown stat by stat as after a battle. The yard can be entered on any free day once the gate's matter is answered, to read the notices.
- In the Tavern whoever is looking for work stands at the counter with a paper laid under them: their skills, what they bring, their stats and how each grows, and a seal that hires them. In the Smithy the price list is on the right and the company on the left: point at a weapon and each companion shows whether they could hold it and what their Attack would become (and whether it is too heavy).
- The arts screen also shows both magic grades and every known spell with uses left; the Barracks shows spell uses (spells are cast in battle); the battle result lists magic grades gained and spells learned.

## Bestiary

Every normal enemy belongs to a band (`EnemyData.band`): early in weeks 1-2, middle in weeks 3-4, late in weeks 5-6 (`RunConfig.BAND_FIRST_DAY`: days 1, 15, 29). Its groups never appear before the band begins, and fronts favour the band of the day (`RunConfig.BAND_AGE_WEIGHT`: older groups still turn up, scaled to the day). Danger 3 fronts from the middle band on can field four enemies.

- **Early (weeks 1-2)**: Orc, Goblin Thief, Goblin Wizard, Stalker, Skeleton Bird*, Winged Zombie*, Slime Monster (soaks blows, re-forms, slows), Briarwing* (thorn mantle reflects 50%).
- **Middle (weeks 3-4)**: Cursed Warrior, Armored Beast, Minotaur, Blackwing* (fast diver), Poisoned Wings* (poisons the line), Flame Eagle* (fire caster, burning), Poison Spawn (heavy poison, corrodes DEF/RES), One-Eye* (debuffer, its gaze adds recovery), Ancient Treant (protector: buffs and heals every enemy).
- **Late (weeks 5-6)**: Stormcrest* (lightning caster), Void Raven* (its caw orders the others to act sooner), Shadowed Wings* (assassin that aims from the left), Faceless Spawn (burner), Cursed Dweller (slow heavy hitter, stacking frenzy), Forgotten Guardian* (wards every enemy, frost aegis reflects).

\* Flying. Large (spears are effective): Orc, Armored Beast, Minotaur, Ancient Treant, Cursed Dweller, and the bosses Mossback Troll, Rampart Golem and The Horned Tyrant. Each enemy's data is in `data/enemies/`, its groups in `data/run/groups/`.

## Bosses

`data/enemies/bosses/` and `data/run/bosses/` (a boss group: the boss, then any escorts, who grow with the day; `description` is the one-line warning). Each boss day draws one from its pool by the run's seed (`Run.boss_draw`). Every boss has one idea:

- **Day 14**: The Horned Tyrant (its War Bellow stacks: it grows stronger the longer the fight runs), Mossback Troll (regenerates 18% of its HP on every Buff it shows: burst it down), Rampart Golem (high DEF, no RES, slow and crushing, braces to halve damage: magic, DEF-ignoring arts and Guard).
- **Day 28**: Rimeclaw Wyvern (flies, frost magic, Take Wing makes it very hard to hit: bows and accuracy), The Oathbreaker (its Oath of Thorns, a Defend, reflects 80% of weapon and art damage: read the intent, use spells), The Fallen Commander (with a One-Eye and a Flame Eagle: rallies every enemy and orders them to act sooner; alone it makes a last stand: choose the kill order).
- **Day 42**: The Faceless Princess (aims from the left and poisons: put the sturdiest on the left), The Forsaken Seraph (flies, magic, charges Judgement on everyone: RES, bows, speed and a delay spell), The Eclipse Sovereign (adds recovery to everyone; at half HP turns faster and harsher).

### Enemy mechanics

All in the battle rules and driven by data (`EnemyActionData`, `EnemyData`, `StatusEffectData`):
- **Damage over time** (`damage_per_tick`): at the end of every turn; Guard does not halve it. It can injure a companion but never removes an injured one. White magic that removes debuffs clears it.
- **Reflection** (`reflect_percent`): a weapon strike or combat art that lands on the bearer deals that share of its damage (at least 1) back to the striker. Spells are exempt.
- **Healing** (`heal_percent`) on the enemy itself, or on every enemy with the ALL_ALLIES targeting (which also spreads buffs).
- **Reversed targeting** (`EnemyData.reversed_targeting` or per action): the position weights mirrored.
- **Orders** (`hasten_allies`): every other enemy's countdown drops by N, never below 1.
- **Recovery pressure** (`add_recovery`): adds to the recovery of each companion reached. A lone companion still ignores recovery.
- **Phases** (`phase_trigger`: at an HP share, or when the last one standing): a second action list, a banner, an optional lasting effect, and the pending action is replaced.
- Also: stacking effects (`max_stacks`) and actions never picked twice in a row (`no_repeat`).

## Tests

```
godot --headless --path . --import          # first time / after adding scripts: builds the class cache
godot --headless --path . -s res://tests/run_tests.gd
```

Exit code 0 means every test passed. `TEST_ONLY=playthrough` runs only tests whose name contains that text (several, comma-separated), `TEST_SKIP=playthrough` skips them; `TEST_VERBOSE=1` prints each test's name. Besides the battle rules (`run_tests.gd`), the run rules (`run_rule_tests.gd`), magic and effectiveness (`magic_tests.gd`), the bestiary, bosses and their mechanics (`bestiary_tests.gd`) the weapon rules (`weapon_tests.gd`: starting stats, the ladder, attack power by type, effectiveness, Large, where weapons come from, two-proficiency weapons) and the training ground (`training_tests.gd`), the run prints the smithy's stock by week, typical damage of every weapon type and tier against the day's average enemy at levels 1, 6 and 12, a random-policy battle win rate, how fast grades rise, and an automated playthrough: a simple policy (`run_policy.gd`) plays 200 whole runs through the rule classes and reports how often it wins, on which day runs end, and for each boss how often it was faced and beaten. The policy answers the mechanics in obvious ways: it never strikes into a reflecting stance, delays a long charged attack on everyone, guards a heavy blow aimed at it, and stands its sturdiest on the left against a boss that aims from the left. On a free day with nothing better to do (after resting, hiring, buying and mending) it trains: a drill in the second type of a two-proficiency weapon owned or in stock that a fielded companion lacks only that grade for and that would raise their Attack (the only time it opens a type from E), else a proficiency drill in the weapon a fielded companion holds (above E), else Sparring for the lowest-level fielded companion.

## Layout

- `scripts/battle/` battle rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, seeded `RandomNumberGenerator` injected. `battle_config.gd` holds every tuning number (grade thresholds, points per action, the per-battle cap, the tier odds per grade). `proficiency.gd` is the grade rules (including whether a companion may equip a weapon), `art_learning.gd` picks the art learned on a grade-up.
- `scripts/run/` the run rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, every random choice from an injected seeded generator. `Run` (the day flow, city, gold and materials, roster and line, today's pending choice, the outcome; commands return false or a reason when not allowed), `RunCalendar` (the weekly pattern and where the events fall), `RunCompanion` (data, progress, HP between days), `Levels` (XP per level and the growth rolls), `RunInventory`/`OwnedWeapon` (the armory: each weapon held by at most one companion), `Front`, `BattleReport`, `Drill`/`DrillResult` (the training ground), and `run_config.gd` with every run tuning number (day counts, rewards, city damage, prices, enemy scaling). `CompanionProgress` is what a companion carries between battles: level, XP and stat gains, proficiency, known and equipped arts, and the weapon in hand; `Battle` reads and updates it.
- `scripts/data/` `Resource` classes for companions, enemies, weapons, combat arts, the art library, spells (`SpellData`) and the spell library, enemy traits (`EnemyTraits`), enemy actions and effects. `scripts/battle/spell_learning.gd` picks the spell learned on a school grade-up; `CombatMath.DamageType` is physical or magic.
- `data/` the `.tres` data: companions (stats, growth rates, weapon, starting grades, signature art), enemies (with their action lists; bosses in `enemies/bosses/`), weapons (`<tier>_<type>.tres`: wooden, bronze, iron, steel, silver and platinum of each of the five types, the 24 two-proficiency weapons `<tier>_<family>.tres`, and the four rare magic weapons), `arts/<weapon type>/` combat arts, `arts/art_library.tres` (every art that can be learned, signature arts included), `spells/black/` and `spells/white/` spells with `spells/spell_library.tres` (every spell that can be learned, the mages' signature spells included), `battles/prototype_battle.tres` (used by the battle tests), and `run/`: `run_content.tres` lists everything a run is built from: the companions, front `sites/`, enemy `groups/` (danger and the days they appear), `bosses/` (one per boss day), `events/`, `shop/` (the rare magic weapons the smithy may stock) `weapons` (the 30-weapon ladder the smithy, events and bosses draw from) and `hybrid_weapons` (the 24 two-proficiency weapons). A new event, group or rare item is a new `.tres` added to that list; no code.
- `scripts/ui/`, `scenes/main.tscn` (the main scene). `game.gd` shows the title (`title_screen.gd`) and then the city. `city_screen.gd` is the hub: it reads the run, sets each building's state, and opens the war table (`war_table.gd`), the party (`party_screen.gd`, which opens `arts_screen.gd`), the smithy and tavern (`store_screen.gd`), letters (`letter_panel.gd`: visits, events, the watch's report, the opening), the battle (`battle_screen.gd`) and the run-end screen over itself. The battle screen plays a battle the run prepared (`Run.start_battle`), emits `finished`, and shows the `BattleReport` the run hands back. Screens only read run and battle state and call their commands.
- The look: things from the world rather than an interface. `ui_theme.gd` holds the colours, the fonts (a book serif; system fonts for now) and the shared drawing (framed boards with cut corners and grain, parchment sheets). `SlantPanel`, `SlantBar`, `ActionButton` and `RowButton` are the shared pieces (the names date from when panels leaned; `UiTheme.SLANT` is 0 now); the two buttons have a `paper` style for lines written on parchment. In battle, `action_menu.gd` is the close-up and the orders (light on a soft-edged veil of shadow, so the art and the field show through; `ActionButton`'s `veil` style) and `party_panel.gd` one portrait of the line; in the Barracks, `muster_figure.gd` is one standing figure, which can be carried and set down. Companion art (`assets/character/`) is a cut-out with no background: wherever a face is shown in a frame or a ring the UI paints a plain ground behind it, and `CompanionData.figure_scale` evens out how large each was painted so heads match side by side (`portrait_focus` says where the face is). `ui_icons.gd` draws the small pictures. `stat_table.gd` writes a companion's stats with their growth lines (the barracks page and a hire's paper use it); `arts_screen.gd` is the drill book, `store_screen.gd` the tavern and the smithy, and `training_screen.gd` the drill yard. After a battle, `battle_result.gd` runs the result (`spoils_card.gd` is one companion's card, `level_up_panel.gd` the stat-by-stat reveal of one level).
- The city is drawn in code, with no image files: `city_art.gd` is the kit (walls, roofs, windows, doors, flames), `city_building.gd` the eight buildings, `city_backdrop.gd` the scene and its times of day (`set_phase`), `interior_backdrop.gd` the inside of a building, `stage_backdrop.gd` the battlefield. Where each building stands is the `RECTS` table in `city_building.gd`. Painted art can replace any of these later without touching the screens.
- The layout is designed at 1920x1080 and scales with the window.
- `tests/` headless tests: `run_tests.gd` (battle rules, and the runner), `run_rule_tests.gd` (run rules and the playthrough), `run_policy.gd` (the automated player), `magic_tests.gd`, `bestiary_tests.gd`, `weapon_tests.gd`, `training_tests.gd`.
- `assets/` character and enemy art.

## Weapons

Six tiers, and every one of the five types (sword, axe, spear, bow, dagger) exists in every tier: 30 weapons (`WeaponData.tier`, `RunContent.weapons`). Mt = the tier's base (wooden 0, bronze 3, iron 6, steel 9, silver 12, platinum 15) + the type's offset (sword 0, spear +1, bow -1, dagger -2; the axe 0 at wooden and bronze, +1 at iron and steel, +2 at silver, +3 at platinum), never below 0 (`BattleConfig.WEAPON_TIER_BASE_MIGHT`, `WEAPON_TYPE_MIGHT_OFFSET`, `WEAPON_TIER_GRADE`).

| Tier (grade) | Sword | Axe | Spear | Bow | Dagger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wooden (E) | 0 / 90 / 4 | 0 / 75 / 8 | 1 / 85 / 6 | 0 / 100 / 3 | 0 / 95 / 1 |
| Bronze (D) | 3 / 90 / 5 | 3 / 75 / 10 | 4 / 85 / 7 | 2 / 100 / 4 | 1 / 95 / 2 |
| Iron (C) | 6 / 90 / 6 | 7 / 75 / 11 | 7 / 85 / 8 | 5 / 100 / 5 | 4 / 95 / 2 |
| Steel (B) | 9 / 85 / 8 | 10 / 70 / 13 | 10 / 80 / 10 | 8 / 95 / 7 | 7 / 90 / 3 |
| Silver (A) | 12 / 90 / 7 | 14 / 75 / 12 | 13 / 85 / 9 | 11 / 100 / 6 | 10 / 95 / 3 |
| Platinum (S) | 15 / 95 / 7 | 18 / 80 / 12 | 16 / 90 / 9 | 14 / 100 / 6 | 13 / 100 / 3 |

Mt / Hit / weight. Axes are the least accurate and heaviest, daggers the lightest, bows and daggers the most accurate; wooden is the lightest tier. Every bow is effective against Flying, every spear against Large.

- **Damage**: attack power + Mt (+ an art's bonus) - the target's DEF, at least 1. Attack power depends on the weapon type: sword and spear STR; axe STR + BLD / 2; bow (STR + DEX) / 2; dagger (STR + SPD) / 2; every half rounded down, from the stats as they stand in battle (after the weight penalty and any effects). A magic weapon uses MAG in place of STR (a magic bow is (MAG + DEX) / 2) and strikes against RES. Enemies (STR or MAG + their action's Mt) and spells (MAG + the spell's Mt) are not affected by weapon type. The rule is `CombatMath.attack_power`; between battles `RunCompanion.attack_power(w)` gives it from the stats a companion carries, and `RunCompanion.attack(w)` adds the weapon's Mt (the "Attack" figure: what a strike deals before DEF).
- **The rare magic weapons** sit beside bronze: Emberglass Sword Mt 5 and Whisper Fang (dagger) Mt 3 need E+; Moonthread Bow Mt 4 and Stormcall Pike Mt 6 need D. Each is 2 Mt above the bronze weapon of its type and strikes with MAG against RES. The Smithy may stock each (15% a week, from week 2, 150 gold); the hermit sells the Emberglass Sword.
- **The Smithy** rolls its stock on the first day of each week: 5 different weapons, each with a tier drawn by the week (`RunConfig.SHOP_TIER_WEIGHTS`): week 1 wooden and bronze; week 2 adds iron; week 3 steel; week 4 a little silver; weeks 5-6 mostly steel, some silver. Each slot is a two-proficiency weapon of that tier with a 20% chance when the tier has them (bronze to silver; `RunConfig.SHOP_HYBRID_CHANCE`), otherwise a ladder weapon of a random type (a slot that rolls a weapon already in stock is drawn again, so about 23% of bronze-silver slots end up two-proficiency). It never sells platinum. Prices by tier: 20, 50, 110, 200, 320, 480 gold; two-proficiency weapons 65, 140, 250, 400 (`Run.weapon_price`). Any rare magic weapon that rolled in comes first; then by tier and type, a two-proficiency weapon after the ladder weapon of its type.
- **Events** that grant a weapon either give a set one or draw one when the event comes up: a tier drawn as the week's stock is, or (one time in four) one tier above the best the Smithy sells that week; then a two-proficiency weapon of that tier (and type) with the same 20% chance. A Wounded Knight leaves a bow; the Ruined Armory any type.
- **Bosses**: beating the day 14 or day 28 boss drops one random weapon (any type) a tier above the best the Smithy sells that week: steel on day 14, platinum on day 28 (`Run.boss_weapon_tier`); on day 14 a two-proficiency steel weapon with the same 20% chance (there is no platinum one). It goes to the armory and the battle's report names it (`BattleReport.weapon_found`). The day 42 boss ends the run and drops nothing.

### Two-proficiency weapons

Six families need a grade in two types. Each **is its main type** (the first named) for everything: the attack power formula, the proficiency gained by using it, which arts can be used with it, effectiveness. The second type is only a requirement: using the weapon never raises it, so it rises only by training (see Training ground; a weapon drill can open a type from E, a school drill cannot) or by fighting with a weapon of that type, or comes from the companion's starting grades.

| Family | Main + second |
|---|---|
| Halberd | axe + spear |
| Glaive | spear + sword (effective against Large) |
| Greatsword | sword + axe |
| Twinblades | dagger + sword |
| Javelin | spear + bow (effective against Large) |
| Runeblade | sword + black magic: a magic weapon (MAG in place of STR, against RES) |

Each family exists at bronze, iron, steel and silver ("Bronze Halberd" ... "Silver Halberd"; `data/weapons/<tier>_<family>.tres`, listed in `RunContent.hybrid_weapons`, 24 in all). To hold one: the tier's grade in the main type and one grade lower in the second, but never below E+ (everyone has E in everything, so E would be no requirement; `BattleConfig.HYBRID_SECOND_GRADE_MIN`): bronze D + E+, iron C + D, steel B + C, silver A + B. Mt is that of the main type's ladder weapon one tier up (`BattleConfig.HYBRID_MIGHT_TIERS_UP`), weight 2 above the main type's weapon of the same tier (`HYBRID_WEIGHT_BONUS`), Hit as it; the price is the tier's x1.25, rounded to 5 (`RunConfig.HYBRID_PRICE_MULT`). Effective only per the main type: Glaive and Javelin against Large, the others against nothing.

| Tier (needs) | Halberd | Glaive | Greatsword | Twinblades | Javelin | Runeblade | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze (D + E+) | 7 / 75 / 12 | 7 / 85 / 9 | 6 / 90 / 7 | 4 / 95 / 4 | 7 / 85 / 9 | 6 / 90 / 7 | 65 |
| Iron (C + D) | 10 / 75 / 13 | 10 / 85 / 10 | 9 / 90 / 8 | 7 / 95 / 4 | 10 / 85 / 10 | 9 / 90 / 8 | 140 |
| Steel (B + C) | 14 / 70 / 15 | 13 / 80 / 12 | 12 / 85 / 10 | 10 / 90 / 5 | 13 / 80 / 12 | 12 / 85 / 10 | 250 |
| Silver (A + B) | 18 / 75 / 14 | 16 / 85 / 11 | 15 / 90 / 9 | 13 / 95 / 5 | 16 / 85 / 11 | 15 / 90 / 9 | 400 |

Mt / Hit / weight. `WeaponData.second_type` (a weapon type) or `second_school` (a magic school), with `second_grade`, hold the second requirement (-1 / unset for ordinary weapons); `WeaponData.requirement_text()` writes it for display ("axe C · spear D"; an ordinary weapon "spear C"). Every check of whether someone can hold a weapon goes through `CompanionProgress.weapon_block_reason(w)` (`can_equip_weapon` is it being empty), which names the first grade missing ("Needs spear D; Elena is E.").

## Combat arts

An art belongs to one weapon type, can only be used with a weapon of that type in hand, and costs stamina and a recovery like any action (always one turn). Tier 1: 4-6 STA, sits out 1 action; tier 2: 8-10 STA, 2 actions; tier 3: 13-15 STA, 3 actions. Every weapon type has 12 arts, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/arts/`, all listed in `art_library.tres`). A companion's signature art is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other user of that weapon can learn it. Effects are limited to bonus Mt/Hit/Crit, striking every enemy, striking twice, ignoring part of DEF, effectiveness against a trait, a debuff on each enemy hit (lowered stats, some of which stack; taking more damage; bleeding or poison every turn, which can kill) and a buff on the user (raised stats; taking less damage; being aimed at more or less often; a stance that answers physical blows: an enemy whose physical action lands on the user is hurt for a share of the damage, at least 1, and can die of it; magic is never answered). `WeaponData.unique_art` is reserved for rare weapons that grant an art of their own; nothing uses it yet, nor `CombatArtData.durability_cost`.
