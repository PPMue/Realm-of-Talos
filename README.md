# Realm of Talos

2D roguelite party builder (Godot 4.7, GDScript). You command the last city and defend it from monsters for 42 days, six weeks. Currently: a playable run (a calendar of battle, free, elite and boss days, with events at the start of some free days) with countdown battles, weapon proficiency, combat arts and magic that carry through the run, a city whose facilities are built up, enchanted weapons, and gemstones set into accessories at the Jeweler.

Every rule and every screen is described in this file. `design/` holds the design notes the larger systems were built from (`traits.md`, `city-and-enchanting.md`, `accessories.md`, and `battle.md`, of which step 1 is built and steps 2 and 3 are proposed); where the two differ, this file is what the game does.

## Run

Open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or from a shell:

```
godot --path .
```

### A run

- The game opens on a title screen (begin a new run, window or full screen, leave). A run opens with a letter to the commander naming the two companions who answered the call.
- The city is the hub, and it is a place: its buildings are the buttons. The **Gate** is where the day's business is (march out on battle and boss days, hear the matter that opens some free days, send a party beyond the walls or end a free day); the **Tavern** hires, the **Smithy** sells weapons, the **Inn** rests the company, the **Mason's Yard** mends the walls, trades one material for the other and builds up the city (always open, to look), the **Barracks** holds the party (always open), and the **Watchtower** reports the days ahead (always open). Buildings that cannot be used today are shuttered and say why; once the day's one action is spent the Inn, the Mason's Yard and the Drill Yard shut, while the Tavern, the Smithy and the Infirmary stay open, since they take only coin. The building to go to next has a glowing name board.
- The sky is the clock. A free day runs from morning to dusk once its action is spent; a battle day opens at dawn with the enemy's fires beyond the wall; a boss day has a red sky. A curtain with the day's number falls between days.
- A thin strip at the top shows the day, the next 7 days as medallions (swords = battle, sun = free day, letter = a free day that begins with an event, horned skull = boss, swords under a stone in violet = an elite fight; a cut stone at a day's rim = the gemstone a win that day brings; a small wagon at a day's rim = the first day of a week, when the smithy and the tavern have something new; hover for details), the walls (city health, 100; at 0 the run is lost), gold, iron ore and timber, and under those the gemstones in hand (each kind held, with how many). The Jeweler's shop stands by the wall at the top left, shuttered until the masons build it; then its window is lit and its board says how many stones wait to be set. The company stands along the bottom as round portraits with their health as an arc; click one to open the barracks on them. A slip of parchment at the bottom left gives the day's news.
- Rules are not written on the screens: they are in tooltips, on the letters that buildings and events show before you commit, and in this file. Keys still work but are not drawn on buttons; a button's tooltip names its key. In the city: P barracks, G gate, W watchtower, Esc puts a letter down.
- 42 days in six weeks of seven. Week 1 goes battle, free, battle, free, free, **elite**, free. The boss weeks (2, 4 and 6) go battle, free, battle, free, battle, free, boss (days 14, 28 and 42). The week after a boss (weeks 3 and 5) is turned round: free, battle, free, battle, free, **elite**, free, so the day after a boss is free (days 15 and 29). The elite fights are on days 6, 20 and 34 (see Elite fights and gemstones). So day 1 is a battle, no two fights are ever on consecutive days, and the days before every boss and every elite are free; the only two free days in a row are days 4 and 5, to get ready for the first elite. A run has 15 battles, 3 elites, 3 bosses and 21 free days (`RunCalendar`: `DayType.ELITE`, `is_elite_day(day)`, `after_boss_week(day)`; `RunCalendar.is_fight(type)` and `Run.is_fight_day(d)` are true for a battle, an elite or a boss).
- Events: one free day in every week begins with an event (six a run), never the day before a boss, and never on an elite or boss day (they are not free days); the day after a boss may have one. The days are drawn when the run begins and show on the strip. An event does not repeat while unseen ones remain.
- The week: the Tavern's offers, the Smithy's stock and the training ground's drills are rolled on the first day of each week (days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36) and stay the same all week; `RunCalendar.days_until_restock(day)` says how many days until the next (7 on day 1, 1 on day 7, -1 in the last week, when none is left). Someone hired or a weapon bought is gone until the next week. What the Smithy sells climbs with the weeks (see Weapons).
- Hiring: a hire costs 100 gold in week 1 and 10 more each week after (week 6: 150; `Run.hire_cost()`, `RunConfig.HIRE_COST`, `HIRE_COST_PER_WEEK`), then less with the Tavern's level 2 and the company's traits, never below 0; one price for everyone that week. Everyone outside the company practises on their own and has one state for the whole run that only grows (`Run.offer_progress(companion)`: what someone offered this week would join with; null for anyone not offered). At every weekly restock after the first (days 8, 15, 22, 29, 36) each of them gains 8-12 proficiency points (`RunConfig.TAVERN_PRACTICE_MIN`, `MAX`), offered or not: three quarters, rounded up (`TAVERN_PRACTICE_MAIN_PERCENT`), in what they fight with (a mage: the school of their own spell; anyone else: the type of their weapon), the rest in one other weapon type or school they already have above E, drawn at random (all of it in the main one if there is none). Each grade reached teaches an art or a spell as in battle; an art learned with every slot full takes the place of a random equipped one or stays unequipped, by chance. A hire joins with that state and their traits, at the roster's average level with the level-ups rolled at the hire. By week 6 the practice comes to about 50 points (the main grade about B, some five arts or spells learned), against up to 8 points a battle for someone fielded from week 1.
- You start with 2 random companions of the fourteen (ten fighters, two for each weapon, and four mages, two for each school; at least one of the two is never a mage). The two who share a weapon are built differently: Elena is all health and strength where Asterion is armour, Aina is balanced where Kaito is strength and crits with no defence, Erik is accurate where Koharu is swift and lucky, Ingrid is sturdy where Raizen is fast, Yuzu is speed and crits where Shiro is tough, Aveline heals where Elio protects, and Freya is a glass cannon where Sylas is slow, hardy and hinders. Everyone starts with 16-24 health (Elena the most, Elio the least) and 12-20 stamina (every fighter more than every mage), and with the wooden weapon of their type; the armory also starts with one wooden weapon of every type the two starters' weapons do not cover (so the company owns every wooden weapon; a hire later brings only their own). Up to 4 are fielded, left to right (rightmost = nearest the enemies); the rest wait in reserve. In the Barracks the company stands in its line behind the muster rail, rear to front, with the reserve behind the bench's board below (the art is cut out, so they stand free against the wall; each stands in a bay of their own between timber posts, and nobody's art covers a neighbour): carry someone to another place or to the bench to move them (someone from the bench set down on a full line changes places with whoever stood there), or use the orders on their page (keys: left and right arrows, F, A). The page on the right is the muster roll for whoever is selected: their stats, each with a line under it as long as its growth rate (the chance it rises by 1 on a level-up, with the figure beside it), what they hold and their Attack with it (attack power for that kind of weapon plus its Mt: what a strike deals before the target's defence; each weapon they could take shows the Attack it would give), and the armory to take a weapon from (a weapon not allowed says why: the grade it needs). It also opens the combat arts screen. All of this is free on any day until you commit to the day's activity.
- Levels: every companion starts at level 1. A won battle grants an XP pool (by danger, growing with the day; shown on each front) shared among the companions fielded in it, injured ones included; reserve companions and lost battles get none. A quarter of the pool is split evenly, so someone who did little still gains a little; the other three quarters are split by the contribution points each earned in that battle: a landed Attack 2 (a miss 1), a combat art used 3, a spell cast 3 (of any kind), each enemy blow that lands on them 1 (2 while guarding), and 4 more for landing the killing blow on an enemy (an art that kills several earns it for each; a kill by bleeding, poison or blight is whoever applied it, a kill by a reflecting stance or ward is whoever carries it). Focus and Wait earn nothing. If nobody earned a point the whole pool is split evenly. Shares are rounded down and the XP left over goes 1 at a time to the highest scorers, so the whole pool is always handed out (`Levels.split_xp`; the points are `BattleConfig.POINTS_*`, the even share `RunConfig.XP_EVEN_SHARE_PERCENT`). On each level-up every stat but BLD rolls its own growth rate (each companion's own, about 40% on average) to rise by 1, and at least 2 stats always rise. Max HP gained raises current HP too. Tavern hires join at the roster's average level, rounded down. A companion fielded in every battle in a party of four who earns an average share ends the run around level 13.
- HP carries over between days. At the end of every day each companion not in the Infirmary heals 20% of max HP (rounded up; some traits change it: see Traits). Stamina is full at the start of every battle. Nobody is ever removed from the company: a companion injured in battle goes to the Infirmary for 4 days (see Infirmary). Healing spells are cast only in battle: between battles the company recovers by the daily heal and by Rest at the Inn.
- **Battle day**: the Gate opens the war table with 2 or 3 fronts (keys 1-3), each with its enemies, a danger of 1-3, the reward and the city health lost if the battle is lost (15/20/25). Fronts you do not pick have no consequence. Enemies get stronger as the days pass. Until the company marches out, the Smithy (buy, enchant, move an enchantment) and the Tavern (hire; a hire joins the line if there is room and can fight that day) work as on a free day (`Run.market_block_reason()`); once a front is committed they close ("Today's battle has already begun."), and an enchanting offer still open is kept as "keep". Everything else (cure, the Mason's Yard, rest, drills, scavenging) keeps to free days (`Run.errand_block_reason()`).
- **Boss day**: one front; the Smithy and the Tavern are open until the march, as on a battle day. Losing ends the run; beating the day 42 boss wins it. Each boss day draws one of three bosses when the run begins (see Bosses). Until the Watchtower is built up the boss days ahead are not marked and their bosses not named (see The city's upgrades). The day strip marks the boss's day with a horned skull, and when the next boss day is beyond the days shown, one more medallion after a gap marks it; hover a boss medallion, or climb the Watchtower, for every boss still ahead with its one-line warning. The boss card shows the boss large, its name, what makes it dangerous and any escorts.
- **Free day**: 1 action, one of: rest at the Inn (everyone not in the Infirmary to full HP and every spell back to full uses; otherwise uses come back only on leaving the Infirmary and, one a night, with the Inn's second level), mend the walls at the Mason's Yard (3 timber for 20 city health; see The city's upgrades), train one companion at the training ground (see Training ground), send a party scavenging beyond the walls (see Scavenging), set a stone at the Jeweler (see Crafting). Besides it, and before or after it, any number of errands that cost gold but never the action: buy at the Smithy (weapons from the week's stock, one of each; see Weapons for what it sells), hire at the Tavern (from the week's 1-2 companions offered, mages included), cure companions in the Infirmary (see Infirmary), build up the city's facilities, swap materials at the Mason's Yard, enchant weapons at the Smithy (see The city's upgrades and Enchanting). Errands also wait for the day's event (`Run.errand_block_reason`). Buying, hiring, enchanting and moving an enchantment are open on battle and boss days too, until the march (see Battle day). End the day at the Gate when done; an unused action is lost.
- **Event**: on a free day that begins with one, a letter with a short story and 2-3 choices (keys 1-3; it can be put down and picked up again at the Gate) that trade gold, materials, city health, companions' HP or a free weapon (a set one, or one drawn when the event comes up with a tier that fits the week; the result names it). It comes first: the buildings stay shut and the day cannot end until it is answered, so every event has a choice that costs nothing the player might lack (no gold, no materials, nobody fit to send: a test checks every event); then the free day goes on with its action as usual. If an event brings the walls to 0, the city falls at once.
- The run is lost when the city reaches 0 or a boss wins.

### Infirmary

- **In battle**: a companion who drops to 0 HP is injured and out of that battle. They do not act; enemies never aim at them (single-target weights are shared among those standing, and a blow already aimed at them is drawn again at once among those standing; an attack on the whole line skips them); every effect on them ends and nothing new lands on them, damage over time included; white magic cannot reach them (a spell on one ally is refused, one on every ally skips them). Nothing in battle undoes an injury. The battle is lost when everyone fielded is injured. The injured still get their XP share of a win and learn what their grades teach.
- **After the battle**, won or lost, everyone injured goes to the Infirmary (`Run.infirmary()`, in the order they went in): they leave the line, cannot be fielded or drilled, and are skipped by Rest, the daily heal and events that touch companions' HP. Their weapon stays in their hand and their progress is untouched; a tavern hire's level still averages the whole roster.
- **The stay**: 4 days (`RunConfig.INFIRMARY_DAYS`; a trait can lengthen it: `Run.infirmary_days`). Injured on day D, they are away days D+1 to D+4 and come out at the start of day D+5 at full HP and with every spell's uses back, on the bench (not into the line). Rest does nothing for them and never shortens it.
- **Cure** (a free-day errand, "Cure": gold only, never the day's action, any number a day): one companion comes out at once, at full HP and with every spell's uses back, onto the bench, and can be fielded the same day. It costs the days still to serve x (4 + 2 x their level) gold (`RunConfig.CURE_GOLD_PER_DAY_BASE`, `CURE_GOLD_PER_DAY_PER_LEVEL`): with 4 days left (the day after the injury) 24 gold at level 1, 56 at level 5, 96 at level 10, 136 at level 15; a level-10 companion with 2 days left costs 48. Days still to serve are the days until they would come out by themselves: 4 the day after the injury, 1 on the last day.
- **Nobody to fight**: on a battle or boss day with everyone in the Infirmary, the day can be given up (`Run.yield_day()`, allowed only then). It counts as a lost battle at the worst front: the city health that front would cost; on a boss day the city falls.

### Training ground

A free-day action (`Run.FreeAction.TRAIN`, "Train"), sharing the day's one action with Rest, Repair and Scavenge: one a day, not on battle or boss days, not before the day's event is answered.

- **The week's drills** (`Run.drills`), posted with the weekly restock: always **Sparring**, plus two proficiency drills, each a different weapon type or magic school drawn at random from all seven subjects (the five weapon types and the two schools), skipping only one in which the whole roster is already at S, so a posted drill is never useless to everyone. With fewer such types, fewer are posted (with none, only Sparring). Weapon drills are listed before school drills. A drill is not used up: the same one can be taken on every free day of the week.
- **Taking one**: pick a drill and a companion in the roster, fielded or on the bench. It costs the day's action and 30 gold (`RunConfig.DRILL_COST`). A proficiency drill can be taken by anyone below S in its weapon type or school, including someone at E with no points: that is how a new weapon type or school is opened (reaching E+ teaches an art or a spell like any grade, so a fighter can become a caster this way: the spell is cast with their MAG, however little, and Rest restores its uses like any other); Sparring needs a level below 20. `Run.drill_block_reason(drill, companion)` says why not; `Run.train(drill, companion)` returns a `DrillResult`. If nobody could learn from any posted drill, the action itself is blocked.
- **Quality** is rolled: poor 25%, good 50%, great 25% (`RunConfig.DRILL_QUALITY_WEIGHTS`).
- **Proficiency drill**: poor 3, good 5, great 8 points toward the next grade (`RunConfig.DRILL_POINTS`), never past S, with no per-battle cap. Good is about one battle's worth: a companion gains 4.8 points a battle on average with their weapon (capped at 8). Every grade reached teaches a random art (weapon) or spell (school) with the same rules as after a battle, right away (a drill is already after the fact); an art is carried only if a slot is free.
- **Sparring**: good = half an even share of the day's danger-2 battle XP pool: pool / 4 / 2 (on day 1: 86 / 8 = 11 XP; day 41: 23); poor = 60% of good, great = 150% (`RunConfig.DRILL_XP_PERCENT`), rounded. Level-ups are rolled as after a battle, and max HP gained raises HP.
- The result (`DrillResult`) carries the drill, the companion, the quality and its word, the gold paid, points gained, grade before and after with each grade reached (and the art or spell it taught), XP gained, and the level-ups in the `BattleReport.level_ups` shape plus the level each rose from and the stats before it.

### Scavenging

A free-day action (`Run.FreeAction.SCAVENGE`, "Scavenge"), sharing the day's one action with Rest, Repair and Train: not on battle or boss days, not before the day's event is answered, and not with nobody fit to go ("Nobody is fit to go.").

- **The day's sites** (`Run.scavenge_sites()`, a `ScavengeSite` each): every free day, when it begins, 2 places beyond the walls are rolled (`RunConfig.SCAVENGE_SITES_PER_DAY`), with different names (thirteen in all: The Burnt Mill, The Drowned Quarter...). None on battle or boss days. Each has a **size** (1 small, 2 medium, 3 large) and a **risk** (low, medium, high), rolled independently, each 40 / 35 / 25 (`SCAVENGE_SIZE_WEIGHTS`, `SCAVENGE_RISK_WEIGHTS`). If everyone available when the day began could not fill either, the first is rolled again with a size that fits, so one site can always be filled when anyone is fit.
- **Places**: one per size point, each testing a different stat, with its role: SPD Scout, STR Breaker, BLD Hauler, DEX Picker, LCK Finder, DEF Guard, RES Ward, MAG Seer. Its **target** is what a typical companion has in that stat on that day: the fourteen companions' average base stat + their average growth rate x (the typical level - 1), the typical level being 1 + 0.27 x (day - 1) (about 5 on day 14, 9 on day 28, 12 on day 42: the automated runs' pace), rounded (`SCAVENGE_STAT_BASE`, `SCAVENGE_STAT_GROWTH`, `SCAVENGE_LEVEL_PER_DAY`; `Run.scavenge_target`); then low risk -1, medium +0, high +2 (`SCAVENGE_RISK_TARGET_SHIFT`). At medium risk: day 1 SPD 8, STR 7, BLD 6, DEX 9, LCK 5, DEF 4, RES 5, MAG 4; day 42 SPD 13, STR 11, BLD 6 (it never grows), DEX 14, LCK 10, DEF 8, RES 9, MAG 7. A companion's stat is their base plus level-up gains (not their weapon's).
- **The haul** (a full one), fixed when the site is rolled and shown: **gold** 40% of the day's danger-1 battle reward (grown by `REWARD_GROWTH_PER_DAY`), x 1 / 1.8 / 2.6 by size and x 1 / 1.5 / 2.2 by risk (day 1: 16 small and low, 92 large and high; day 42: 30 and 170); **materials** 0-1 / 1-2 / 2-3 pieces by size, each timber or iron ore at random; a **weapon** with 0% / 10% / 25% by risk, drawn like an event's random weapon (any type); and **XP** to each sent, 30% of an even share of the day's danger-1 battle pool with a full line (4 on day 1, 9 on day 42). Numbers: `SCAVENGE_GOLD_*`, `SCAVENGE_MATERIALS_BY_SIZE`, `SCAVENGE_WEAPON_CHANCE`, `SCAVENGE_XP_SHARE`.
- **Sending a party** (`Run.scavenge(site, party)`, `party` indexed by place): every place filled ("Fill every place."), each by a different companion of the company not in the Infirmary, from the line or the bench. `Run.scavenge_block_reason(site, party)` says why not.
- **The chance** of a full haul (`Run.scavenge_chance`, shown before committing, also for a party still being chosen): 60 / 45 / 30 by risk, plus the average of the places' scores (`Run.scavenge_slot_score`): meeting the target +15 and +3 a point above, at most +30; short of it -6 a point, at most -30; an empty place -30. Rounded, then held to 25-95 (`SCAVENGE_BASE_CHANCE`, `SCAVENGE_MEET` and the others).
- **The outcome**, one roll of 1-100: at or under the chance a **Success** (the full haul); otherwise half the time **Passable** (half a haul: half the gold and XP, materials halved rounded down, no weapon), else **Failure** (nothing) (`SCAVENGE_HALF_CHANCE`; the names are `ScavengeResult.OUTCOME_NAMES`). Low risk costs nothing more. Medium risk: on nothing everyone sent loses 25% of max HP (rounded up, never below 1 HP; `SCAVENGE_HP_LOSS_FRACTION`). High risk: on half a haul everyone sent loses 25%; on nothing the one furthest short of their place's target (a tie, or nobody short: one of them at random) is injured and goes to the Infirmary exactly as after a battle, and the others lose 25%.
- XP brings level-ups as after a battle (max HP gained raises HP). The result (`ScavengeResult`) carries the site, the party, the chance and the roll, the outcome, the gold, materials and weapon brought (already in the run and the armory), who was injured, and for each one sent (`members`) the XP gained, HP lost, level and stats before, and the level-ups in the `DrillResult.level_ups` shape.

### Traits

A trait is something a companion is (`TraitData`; `RunCompanion.traits`, the same list in `CompanionProgress.traits` and so in battle): rolled when they first appear and never changed. The design, with every trait's wording and number: `design/traits.md`. Traits are built in waves; only those in the library (`TraitLibrary`, `data/traits/trait_library.tres`) are ever handed out. **Live now (all three waves, 152 traits):** wave 1, every trait that is a number with a condition: all of weapon hands (25: Sure, Heavy, Keen, Easy, Apt for each weapon), schools of magic (8), body and mind (11), two-edged (22) and the line (13); of tempo the ten damage and Hit lines (First Blood, Patient, Closer, Opener, Giant-Bane, Fowler, Spell-Breaker, Grudge, Counter-Eye, Kingslayer); of learning the ten Growing traits, Early Bloomer and Late Bloomer. Wave 2, the single moments: the rest of tempo (8), all of staying alive (12), the rest of learning (Quick Study, Glory-Hound, Bench Learner, Yard Rat, Second Trade, Apprentice, Deep Well), and of the city all but the three scavenging traits (11). Wave 3, other people and scavenging: the company (10) and Pathfinder, Sure-Footed and Pack Mule.

- **Handing out** (`Run._roll_traits`, numbers in `RunConfig.TRAIT_*`): 1, 2 or 3 traits at 75 / 22 / 3%; each drawn by rarity, common 10, uncommon 5, rare 1; never two of the same line of a family (Sword: Sure and Axe: Sure share the line "Sure"; Black: Deep and White: Deep share "Deep"; the ten Growing traits share one; every other trait is a line of its own), never two two-edged ones. No trait is on two people in a run. A trait tied to a weapon type or school goes to someone who can already use it (a grade above E in it) 4 times in 5: when one is drawn for someone who cannot, 80% of the time it is swapped for one of the same line they can use (or any tied one they can); the fifth time it stays where it fell. The two starters roll theirs when the run begins; each tavern offer rolls when the week's offers are rolled (`Run.offer_traits(companion)` shows them before hiring) and a hire keeps them. A companion's traits are rolled once a run, the first time they are offered (from their starting progress, `CompanionProgress.create`): offered again in a later week they show the same ones and nothing new is rolled, and they stay out of the pool whether hired or not, so "no trait on two people" counts everyone seen at the tavern. All with the run's generator.
- **Words**: Hit and Crit are percentage points on the chance to hit or to strike critically (added before the chance is held to its limits); damage is added to each blow after DEF or RES (and an effective strike's half again), before a critical doubles it, never below 1; Avoid +10 means enemies have 10 less Hit against them; drawn more / less multiplies how often enemies aim at them when an aim is drawn (it never moves one already drawn). Places in the line are counted as in the barracks: the rear is the leftmost, the front the rightmost; a line of one is both; middle is neither. "Their first action", "the first three", "from the fourth on" count the companion's own actions in the battle (Guard and Focus included). "Neighbours" are those either side in the line, "behind" the one to their left; such a trait works while its holder is standing.
- **Where they count**, always through the same functions the screens read:
  - Plain stat bonuses (Hale, Strong, Ox...) are in `CompanionProgress.stats()` (so max HP, stamina, the Attack in the barracks and smithy, the weight penalty and scavenging all include them); `trait_stat_bonus(stat)` says how much of a stat comes from traits. A stat bonus that needs the battle (Proud, Vanguard, Lone Blade, Last Stand, Sprinter's Speed) is added by `CompanionUnit.stat` in battle only.
  - Hit, Crit, damage and cannot-miss: in `Battle.forecast` and `Battle.spell_forecast` (so the forecast shows them); weapon-hand traits count with that weapon type in hand (attacks and arts), school traits with that school's spells, the rest with every strike, black spells included. Avoid and the damage a companion takes from enemy blows (Anchor, Shieldmate, Sidestep, Wall): `Battle.enemy_forecast`. Aim: `Battle.target_weights`.
  - Art stamina (Easy, Hothead, Thrifty): `Battle.art_cost(c, art)`, never below 0. Spell uses (Deep): `CompanionProgress.max_uses(spell)`, which Rest refills to. Heals (Kind): `Battle.heal_amount`; on one target (Faithless): `Battle.heal_amount_on`. Lasting: a white spell's buff they cast lasts 1 turn longer.
  - Apt (+50% points): weapon points in battle by half again, fractions carried to the next action, and the battle's cap (`Battle.proficiency_cap`) half again (8 to 12); magic points 4 to 6 a cast, cap 12 to 18 (`Battle.magic_cap`); a drill of that type or school, `Run.drill_points` (good: 5 to 8, rounded).
  - Growth: `Levels.growth_rate_of(progress, stat)` (Growing +15, Early and Late Bloomer by the level being reached: up to 8, or 9 and above), used by every level-up; `Levels.growth_rate` stays the companion's own.
  - XP (Thin-Blooded +15%, Quick Study +10%): every share of a battle, Sparring (`Run.drill_xp_for`) and scavenging, rounded down (`Levels.with_traits`); a report's XP is what they got. The Infirmary stay (`Run.infirmary_days`; the cure price follows the days left) and the day's-end heal (`Run.day_heal`, Daredevil half).
- **Single moments (wave 2)**, each where the moment happens:
  - In battle: Focus (`Battle.focus_amount`, Steady Breath); after each of their actions (Tireless: +1 stamina, never past full); an art's recovery (`Battle.art_recovery`, Quick Hands on their first art of the battle); felling an enemy (Bloodrush stamina, Blood-Drinker HP, Glory-Hound's kill points counted twice towards the XP share, Relentless: that action has no recovery); a critical hit (Staggering Blow: the target's next action 1 turn later, refused once that action was already delayed, as for spells); the first time they fall below half Health (Second Wind: stamina full); a blow that would fell them (Clings to Life: left at 1, once a battle; damage over time is not a blow); enemy blows (`Battle.enemy_forecast`: Thick Hide physical -1, Spell-Scarred magic -1, Brace half for the first blow that lands on them, Stubborn Avoid +15 at 5 Health or less); damage over time (Clean Blood: none); a harmful effect landing on them (Calm: 1 turn shorter, at least 1); white magic on them (Good Patient, `Battle.heal_amount_on`).
  - After a battle: Field Dresser heals 15% of max HP if won and still standing; Looter +10 gold if won, Bounty Hunter +4 gold for each enemy they felled if won (like a weapon's gold for kills); Woodsman +1 timber and Prospector +1 iron ore for everyone who fought, won or lost; Night Watch, on the bench, takes 3 off the walls' loss of a lost battle. All in the report: `BattleReport.trait_gold` (in `gold`), `materials`, `city_damage`, and one line each in `trait_lines` ("Elena, Looter: +10 gold").
  - The day's end (`Run.day_heal`): Fast Healer heals 35% instead of 20% and Camp Cook gives everyone 5 percentage points more; percentage points add up (with the Inn's 30%, Fast Healer heals 45%), then Daredevil halves.
  - Learning: Yard Rat (a poor drill comes out good for them); Second Trade (+50% points in the weapon type of their second-best grade, by points, a tie going to the type listed first, in battle and drills); Apprentice (arts and spells are drawn as if the grade were one letter higher); Deep Well (+1 use of every spell); Bench Learner (a quarter of an even share of a won battle's XP from the bench, `BattleReport.reserve_xp`).
  - The city's traits work while their holder is in the company and not in the Infirmary, line or bench: the smithy's price (`Run.shop_price(item)`, Haggler -10%), a hire (`Run.hire_cost()`, Well-Liked -20), a drill (`Run.drill_cost()`, Drillmaster -10), a repair (`Run.repair_amount()`, Mason's Kin +5), an event choice that costs gold (`Run.choice_gold(choice)`, Silver Tongue -20%). Several holders add up. Healer's Favourite halves their own cure (`Run.cure_cost`).
  - **With the city's upgrades** they add together, with the floors stated: a hire in week 1 100 - 25 (Tavern level 2) - 20 (Well-Liked) = 55, never below 0; a repair 30 (Mason's Yard level 1) + 5 = 35; the overnight heal 30% (Inn level 1) + 15 = 45%; the stay 3 (Infirmary level 1) - 1 = 2 days, never below 1; the cure price -25% (Infirmary level 2) - 50% = a quarter, never below 0; the reserve's XP a quarter (Barracks level 1) + a quarter (Bench Learner) = half an even share; Yard Rat and the Drill Yard's level 1 do the same thing, so together they are no more than either.
- **Other people (wave 3)**: Inspiring (neighbours +5 Hit), Watchful (neighbours Avoid +10), Envied (neighbours +25% points, in battle) and Standard-Bearer (every other companion in the line +3 Hit) count while their holder is standing, through the same forecasts (`TraitModifier.Who`). Battle-Sister / Battle-Brother: +1 Strength and Defence for each standing neighbour, in battle (`CompanionUnit.stat`). Avenger: +3 damage once anyone else in the line has been injured this battle. Rival: +10 Crit while another companion has felled more enemies this battle. Lead by Example: after they fell an enemy, the next action by anyone else in the line has +15 Hit (in its forecast; `Battle.next_ally_hit`). Martyr: when they are injured, everyone still standing heals 5. Mentor: after a won battle, the lowest-level companion in the line (the first of them in line order) gets +20% XP for each other companion in it who is a Mentor.
- **Scavenging (wave 3)**: Pathfinder counts as 3 higher in whatever their place asks (`Run.scavenge_stat`, so the chance shown includes it). Sure-Footed is never the one injured: the injury falls on the one furthest short among the others, and if all are Sure-Footed nobody is (they still lose their 25%). Pack Mule: the party brings back 25% more gold and materials (each rounded; two of them 50%).

### The city's upgrades

Timber builds the city, iron ore forges arms. Both come from battles won (a share of fronts), the bosses (3 of each), events and scavenging; `Run.materials_taken` keeps the count. Measured over the 200 automated runs before upgrades existed, per run still going that week: week 1 about 3 timber and 3 ore, week 2 6 and 6 (the first boss), week 3 3 and 3, week 4 5 and 5 (the second boss), week 5 2 and 2, week 6 1 and 1.5; a run that reaches day 42 takes in about 22 timber and 23 iron ore.

- **Repairs** (the day's action, at the Mason's Yard) cost 3 timber and no ore for 20 city health (`Run.repair_cost`, `Run.repair_amount`).
- **Swap** at the Mason's Yard: 2 of one material for 1 of the other, any number of times (`Run.swap_block_reason(from, to)`, `Run.swap(from, to)`; `RunConfig.SWAP_GIVE`, `SWAP_GET`).
- **Ten facilities**, two levels each, level 1 before level 2 (`Run.Facility`: Watchtower, Smithy, Tavern, Inn, Infirmary, Drill Yard, Barracks, Mason's Yard, Gate, Jeweler; `Run.facility_level`, `Run.facility_lines(f, level)` for each level's line). The Smithy, the Gate and the Jeweler are paid in iron ore, the rest in timber: level 1 costs 3, level 2 costs 5 (`RunConfig.UPGRADE_COST`, `FACILITY_MATERIAL`; `Run.upgrade_cost(f)`). Set so the automated player, taking materials whenever offered and repairing first, builds about 6 of the 20 in a run (`tests/run_policy.gd`).
- Upgrading, swapping, enchanting and moving an enchantment are errands: any number, never the day's action. Upgrading and swapping are on free days once the day's event is answered (`Run.upgrade_block_reason(f)`, `Run.upgrade(f)`); enchanting and moving an enchantment are at the Smithy, so on battle and boss days too until the march (`Run.market_block_reason()`). An upgrade works from the moment it is paid for.

| Facility | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Watchtower | The boss days and which boss comes are known | Tomorrow's fronts are known a day early |
| Smithy | Opens enchanting | The smith offers two enchantments to choose from |
| Tavern | One more hire offered each week | Hires cost 25 gold less |
| Inn | Everyone heals 30% overnight instead of 20% | Every spell gets one use back each night |
| Infirmary | Stays are 3 days instead of 4 | Cures cost a quarter less |
| Drill Yard | Drills never come out poor | A third drill is posted each week |
| Barracks | The reserve gets a quarter share of a battle's XP | The reserve heals fully overnight |
| Mason's Yard | Repairs mend 30 instead of 20 | The walls hold 120 instead of 100 |
| Gate | A third scavenging site each day | A lost front costs the walls 5 less |
| Jeweler | Opens crafting | The jeweler shows two designs to choose from |

- **Watchtower**: without level 1 the run does not tell which days ahead are boss days or which boss comes: `Run.boss_days_known()` is false, `Run.upcoming()` and `Run.seen_day_type(d)` show a boss day ahead as a battle day, `Run.boss_on(d)` is null for a day ahead and `Run.boss_days_ahead()` holds at most today (the boss day itself, and its boss, are seen when it comes). Level 2: on every day before a battle or boss day, tomorrow's fronts are rolled when the day begins (or when level 2 is bought) and `Run.tomorrow_fronts()` returns them; they are the fronts that day brings.
- **Tavern** level 1: the week's offers are 2-3; bought mid-week, one more is offered at once (with their traits). Level 2: `Run.hire_cost()` is 25 less (75 in week 1).
- **Inn** level 1: `Run.day_heal` uses 30%. Level 2: at every day's end each companion not in the Infirmary gets one use back of every spell they know, never past full.
- **Infirmary** level 1: `Run.infirmary_days` is 3 (traits still add); stays already running end a day sooner (not before tomorrow). Level 2: `Run.cure_cost` x 0.75, rounded.
- **Drill Yard** level 1: a poor drill comes out good. Level 2: three proficiency drills a week; bought mid-week, one more is posted at once.
- **Barracks** level 1: after a won battle everyone on the bench gets a quarter of an even share (pool / 4 / 4, rounded; traits apply), in `BattleReport.reserve_xp`, level-ups in `level_ups`. Level 2: at the day's end everyone on the bench is at full HP.
- **Mason's Yard** level 1: a repair mends 30. Level 2: `Run.max_city_health()` is 120 (repairs and events go up to it).
- **Gate** level 1: three scavenging sites a day; bought on a free day, a third is rolled at once. Level 2: `Run.front_city_damage(danger)` is 10 / 15 / 20 (fronts rolled after, and tomorrow's if already rolled).

### Enchanting

At the Smithy once it has its level 1 (an errand, see above: on any day until the march). Any weapon the company owns, in hand or in the armory (`OwnedWeapon`). A weapon is plain or enchanted ("Iron Sword+"); there are no levels.

- **The budget** (`Run.enchant_budget(w)`): the net stat points an enchantment adds up to, by tier: wooden 1, bronze 2, iron 3, steel 4, silver 5, platinum 6; a rare weapon 4 (`RunConfig.ENCHANT_BUDGET_BY_TIER`, `ENCHANT_RARE_BUDGET`).
- **A roll** (`Run.roll_enchant(budget)`): each point is +1 to a stat drawn at random from all eleven (Health, Stamina, Strength, Magic, Critical, Dexterity, Speed, Defence, Resistance, Luck, Build); the same stat can come up again, and some are duds. A quarter of the rolls (`RunConfig.ENCHANT_TWO_EDGED_PERCENT`), and only with a budget of 2 or more, are two-edged: one stat drawn at random takes -k, k drawn evenly from 1 to half the budget (rounded down), and k more points are rolled, none of them on that stat. The net is always the budget; at most one stat is negative.
- **An attempt** (`Run.enchant(w)`, `Run.enchant_block_reason(w)`) costs 1 iron ore and 20 gold x the tier (at least 1: wooden and bronze 20, iron 40 ... platinum 100), a rare weapon 1 ore and 60 gold (`Run.enchant_cost(w)`: materials and "gold"; `RunConfig.ENCHANT_ORE`, `ENCHANT_GOLD_PER_TIER`, `ENCHANT_RARE_GOLD`), first try or again: it does not rise. It pays and makes an offer (`EnchantOffer`, kept in `Run.enchant_offer`): what the weapon has now (`current`, empty if plain) and one new roll, two with the Smithy's level 2 (`rolls`; `RunConfig.ENCHANT_ROLLS_BY_SMITHY`). The player takes a new roll (`Run.take_enchant(index)`) or keeps what the weapon has (`Run.keep_enchant()`; a plain weapon stays plain); the cost is spent either way. While an offer is open nothing else can be enchanted or moved; ending the day or marching out keeps what the weapon has.
- `OwnedWeapon.enchant_stats` (stat -> amount, one may be negative; empty when plain), `is_enchanted()`, `title()` ("Iron Sword+"), `enchant_lines()` ("+2 Strength", "-1 Luck": positives in stat order, the negative last; `OwnedWeapon.stat_lines(stats)` writes any roll the same way).
- **Held**, the enchantment is in the holder's stats (`CompanionProgress.stats()`, so max HP, the Attack, the weight penalty, scavenging and the battle all include it); `CompanionProgress.enchant_stat_bonus(stat)` says how much of a stat comes from it (negative for the two-edged one). Taking an enchantment raises the holder's HP by any Health it adds over what the weapon gave; put down, it stays with the weapon and HP is held to the new max. `RunCompanion.attack_with(owned_weapon)` gives the Attack with another owned weapon, its enchantment included.
- **Moving an enchantment** (`Run.move_enchant(from, to)`, `move_enchant_block_reason`, `move_enchant_cost()`): 2 iron ore moves a weapon's whole enchantment to a plain owned weapon of the same type whose budget is at least as large.

- **Jeweler** level 1: crafting (see Crafting). Level 2: each craft shows two different designs to choose from.

### Elite fights and gemstones

- **An elite day** (days 6, 20 and 34) plays as a battle day: the Gate's war table, the Smithy and the Tavern open until the march, one front must be fought (or the day given up when nobody can march). It has exactly two fronts, both of the highest danger (3), against two different groups where the day has two: danger 3's enemies, gold, XP and material odds, and its cost to the walls when lost (`Front.is_elite`). The Watchtower never hides an elite day, and its level 2 shows tomorrow's elite fronts as it does a battle's.
- **Gemstones** (`Gem`): five, ranks 1 to 5, Topaz, Sapphire, Emerald, Ruby, Diamond (`Gem.NAMES`, `Gem.name_of(rank)`). The company keeps them like materials (`Run.gems`: rank -> count, `Run.gem_count(rank)`, `Run.gem_total()`, `Run.add_gem(rank)`, `Run.gems_taken`). Their only use is to be set into an accessory at the Jeweler (see Crafting).
- **The big fights** before the last boss each give the next stone when won (`Run.gem_for_fight(day)`, shown before the choice as `Front.gem`, given as `BattleReport.gem`; 0 means none): day 6 elite Topaz, day 14 boss Sapphire, day 20 elite Emerald, day 28 boss Ruby, day 34 elite Diamond. The day-42 boss gives none.
- **The current rank** (`Run.gem_rank_now()`): the stone of the last big fight whose day has passed, won or not; Topaz before day 6 (so Topaz to day 14, Sapphire on days 15-20, Emerald 21-28, Ruby 29-34, Diamond from 35). Other sources give stones by it:
  - **Scavenging**: a Success brings back one stone of the current rank RunConfig.SCAVENGE_GEM_PERCENT (3) percent of the time (`ScavengeResult.gem`).
  - **Events** (`EventChoiceData.gem_stone`: NONE, CURRENT, or ONE_LOWER, never below Topaz; `Run.choice_gem(choice)` says which stone; "{gem}" in the result text names it): The Gem Cutter's Widow sells the current stone for 60 gold or one a rank lower for 2 iron ore; in The Old Mine the healthiest companion goes down for the current stone and loses 10 HP.

### Crafting

- **At the Jeweler** once it has its level 1: setting a stone is the day's free action on a free day (`Run.FreeAction.CRAFT`, "Craft"), once the event is answered. It costs the stone and 20 gold x its rank: Topaz 20 ... Diamond 100 (`Run.craft_cost(rank)`: "gem" and "gold"; `RunConfig.CRAFT_GOLD_PER_RANK`).
- **An attempt** (`Run.craft_block_reason(rank)`, `Run.craft(rank)`) pays the stone, the gold and the action and shows the designs (`CraftOffer` in `Run.craft_offer`: `rank`, `designs`): one type drawn evenly from all 54, two different ones with the Jeweler's level 2 (`RunConfig.CRAFT_DESIGNS_BY_JEWELER`). The same type can come up again in a run. `Run.take_craft(index)` makes it (an `Accessory`) and puts it in the company's keeping, unworn; an offer still open when the day ends makes the first design. While an offer is open nothing else can be crafted.
- An accessory is never broken up, sold, upgraded or re-made.

### Accessories

- **An accessory** (`Accessory`) is a type (`AccessoryTypeData`, one of 54 in eight families: `AccessoryLibrary`, res://data/accessories/) set with a stone (`rank`, Gem 1-5): `title()` "Topaz Ring", `lines()`. The stone sets how strong it is, the type what it does. A type's lines: `gives_line(rank)` ("+3 Strength"), `takes_line()` ("" unless two-edged: "-10 Hit"), `lines(rank)`, `is_two_edged()`, `family` (`AccessoryTypeData.Family`: Plain, Striking, Standing, Breath and tempo, Magic, The line, The road, Two-edged). Every type and its five numbers are in design/accessories.md; the files are written by tools/generate_accessories.gd.
- **Wearing** (`Run.accessories`: everything owned; `Run.accessories_of(c)`: worn, in the order put on; `Run.wearer_of(a)`, `Run.unworn_accessories()`, `Run.wear_block_reason(c, a)`, `Run.wear(c, a)`, `Run.take_off_block_reason(a)`, `Run.take_off(a)`): at most two each (`RunConfig.MAX_ACCESSORIES`), never two of one type; free, any number of times, whenever the party can be changed (not once a front is committed). Putting one on takes it from whoever wore it. Someone in the Infirmary keeps theirs on, and it can be taken off them. A hire arrives with none.
- **What it does** counts wherever traits count: each accessory is worked out as a trait for its rank (`Accessory.effect()`, its modifiers' amounts set by the stone: `AccessoryEffect`), and the rules read a companion's traits and accessories together (`CompanionProgress.all_traits()`, `RunCompanion.all_traits()`, `CompanionUnit.all_traits()`): the stats (`CompanionProgress.stats()`; `accessory_stat_bonus(stat)` says how much of a stat comes from them), the battle, growth, XP, spell uses, scavenging, the day's-end heal, gold after a battle. Health from a Pendant works as an enchantment's: put on, HP rises by it; taken off, HP is held to the new max. Spell uses likewise (a Tiara).
- **The new rules**:
  - **Pin**: their weapon strikes and arts ignore that share of the target's DEF (RES with a magic weapon), added to the weapon's or art's own, at most 100%. Not spells.
  - **Thorn**: an enemy whose blow lands on them takes that much damage and can be felled by it (the kill is theirs); not from blows that strike the whole line, nor from a miss.
  - **Talisman**: damage over time on them is cut by that share, rounded down; at Diamond none.
  - **Locket**: once a battle, a blow that would fell them leaves them at 1 Health (Topaz) or 10 / 20 / 30 / 40% of max Health rounded up, even from 1 Health; with Clings to Life the higher figure counts and both are used up (`Battle.survive_hp(c)`).
  - **Hourglass**: the first of their strikes or spells to hit in a battle pushes the target's next action back 1-5 turns, shown as a spell's delay (DELAYED; an action already delayed is not delayed again).
  - **Sigil**: more damage against a boss, and in an elite fight against every enemy (`Battle.elite`, set from `Front.is_elite`).
  - **Spur**: their first 1-5 actions of a battle have 1 less recovery where the action's own recovery is 3 or more (a tier 2 or 3 art or spell), so never below 2 (`TraitModifier.recovery_at_least`; `Battle.action_recovery`, `Battle.art_recovery`). An action of recovery 2 (an attack, Guard, Focus, a tier 1 art or spell) is unchanged but still counts as one of the first. The cap below still holds.
  - **Bell** also gives Defence (+1 / 1 / 2 / 2 / 3); **Poultice** heals 4-20 percentage points of max Health more at the day's end (`DAY_HEAL_SHARE`); **Compass** also brings back 10-50% more gold and materials for the whole party (`SCAVENGE_HAUL_PERCENT`).
  - **Tiara**: more uses of every tier I spell. **Crown**: every spell has 1 fewer use, never below 1.
  - **Cameo**: with more than one thing that changes Guard (the Wall trait), the largest share counts.
  - **Talon** counts against an enemy that is Flying or Large, once.

### Turns, Guard and the retreat

- **Recovery**: after their own action a companion sits out that many of the following actions: Attack, Guard and Focus 2 (`BattleConfig.RECOVERY_*`), an art or a spell 2 / 3 / 4 by tier (its own `recovery`), changed by traits and accessories. **Nobody sits out longer than there are others standing** (`Battle.recovery_cap(c)`): with one other at most 1, with two at most 2, alone 0, so two alternate, three take turns and four have one to spare. `Battle.action_recovery` and `Battle.art_recovery` return the final number, cap included. Recovery an enemy adds (`add_recovery`) comes on top and is not held to it. When someone is injured, every other standing companion's recovery is cut to the new cap at once (an enemy's included). A lone companion still ignores recovery altogether (`Battle.is_lone_companion`). Wait is offered only when nobody is ready, which now takes an enemy's added recovery.
- **Guard** (`Battle.guard(c, ward)`): half damage until the turn ends. Aimed at a standing neighbour (`Battle.guard_wards(c)`: themselves first, then the places either side in the line; an injured one is not stepped over), the guard also covers them this turn: every single-target enemy action that would land on the ward lands on the guard instead, worked out against the guard (their DEF or RES, Avoid, traits) and halved by the Guard, its effect and added recovery on the guard, a reflecting stance or Thorn answering as for any blow on them, a "cover" event (`guard`, `ward`, `enemy`, `action`) before it, and the guarding points for a blow taken. Blows on the whole line are not caught. The cover ends with the turn, or at once when either is injured. The "guard" event carries `ward` (null for themselves). `Battle.guard_preview(c, ward)`: what the guard would take this turn guarding that ward (only actions due this turn, every blow landing, none critical, the Guard's halving counted): `blows` (`enemy`, `action`, `whole_line`, `forecast`), `damage` (with their damage over time), `falls`, and `ward_damage` / `ward_falls` for the ward with the cover up.
- **Sound the retreat** (`Battle.can_retreat()`, `Battle.retreat()`): in a battle or an elite fight, never a boss's (`Battle.allow_retreat`, set by `Run.start_battle`), at any time, the first turn included. No turn passes and nothing lands; the battle is lost at once (`Battle.retreated`, a "retreat" event) and what it taught is learned as on any end. The run treats it as any lost battle (the walls pay, it counts as lost, no XP), but only those already injured go to the Infirmary; the others keep the Health they have. `BattleReport.retreated` says so.

### Battles

- The company is the row of portraits along the bottom; the rightmost stands nearest the enemies and is aimed at most. Down the left of each portrait is what the enemy has aimed at them, in the order it lands: the turns left and the damage of each; the blow they would fall to, if everything before it lands, is in red under a skull and their frame is red. The small roundel at the right is how often an enemy picks that place when it next chooses whom to strike. Someone who has just acted is dimmed under an hourglass with the number of actions until they are ready again, the same picture an enemy's countdown uses; someone injured is reddened and sunk. Click anyone who is ready to choose who acts: they step up out of the line, and their portrait fills the left edge of the screen.
- Give an order from the list beside them, or press its number: Attack, the companion's equipped combat arts (up to 3), Magic (for anyone who knows a spell), Guard, Focus (restores 5 stamina, about a tier 1 art's cost). Point at an order to read what it does under the list; an art you cannot afford is greyed out and says why.
- Magic changes the list to the companion's spells, each with its school, tier and uses left (number keys; Back, Esc or right-click returns; more than six spells page with More). A spell with no uses left is greyed out and says why.
- For Attack and single-target arts, point at an enemy to see hit %, damage and crit %, then click it to strike. Arts that strike every enemy light up every enemy at once; click any of them to confirm. Right-click or Esc backs out.
- A spell aimed at a companion is cast by clicking their portrait below; each portrait shows what it would do (for a heal, HP before and after). A spell for every ally or every enemy lights them all up; one click confirms.
- Under each enemy: its HP, an hourglass with the turns left until it acts, the coming action by name in the colour of its kind, and under that who it is aimed at, the damage and the chance to hit as things stand now ("SYLAS 19 DMG 90% HIT"; "EVERYONE 19-22 DMG" for a blow on the whole line; in red when the blow alone would fell its target), then what else it does. A thread runs from an enemy to whoever it aims at for every blow that lands this turn (in red), and for whatever is pointed at: point at an enemy to see whom it aims at (their frames light), at a companion to see who aims at them. When an aim moves (its target fell, someone drew the eye or was hidden) the enemy pops TURNS ON and the name. Every action you take, art, spell or not, is one turn (the code and the data files call a turn a `tick`). A pending action already pushed back by a spell shows its countdown in pale blue with a DELAYED tag; one brought forward by another enemy's order shows it in orange with a HASTENED tag (and pops SOONER -N).
- Traits such as FLYING show as a tag above an enemy's name (hover it). The forecast says EFFECTIVE when the strike deals half as much again, and MAGIC when it is against RES.
- Lasting effects show on the unit with the turns left (on party panels under the status tag; poison and burning in green-yellow). Poison pops its damage at the end of each turn.
- A reflecting stance wraps the enemy in a ring of pink thorns with a REFLECTS N% tag; the forecast turns pink and says what striking costs you (normal and critical). Spells pass through.
- An enemy that aims from the left carries an AIMS LEFT tag. The party panels' "targeted N%" then counts it: with only such enemies the figure is the mirrored one ("aims from the left"); with both kinds the panel shows both ("targeted N% · from the left N%").
- A second phase gets the big banner (PHASE TWO, its name and one line) and a tag under the enemy. Recovery added by an enemy pops +N RECOVERY on the panel; an enemy's heal pops +N on it.
- When nobody is ready, Wait (1 or Space) lets a turn pass. L opens the battle log. Clicking during playback speeds it up.
- Hold Shift while pointing at an enemy or a companion for what is known about them: stats, grades, arts, traits and what is on them now; for an enemy, everything it can do (each action's name, the turns it takes, whom it strikes, its Mt and Hit and what it leaves); for a companion, what is coming at them, blow by blow, and the turn they would fall. Let go of Shift, or move off them, and the sheet goes. Nothing of the kind pops up on hover alone.

Proficiency and arts:

- Each companion has a grade in every weapon type: E, E+, D, D+, C, C+, B, B+, A, A+, S. Attacking gives 1 point in the type of the weapon held, a combat art 2; Guard, Focus and Wait give none. One companion can gain at most 8 points per battle. The grade and progress for the weapon held show under the companion's name on the left.
- Every grade reached teaches one random art of that weapon type the companion does not know yet (higher grades roll higher tiers). In battle the grade rises at once and a banner announces it, but the art is learned only when the battle is over (won or lost, by everyone fielded, injured ones too), in the order the grades were reached, so nothing new can be used in the battle it was earned in. It is carried (equipped) if one of the three slots is free; otherwise it is known and left behind, and can be swapped for a carried one (`CompanionProgress.swap`). `Battle.learn_after_battle()` does this; the battle calls it itself when it ends.
- Weapons need a grade to be held: wooden E, bronze D, iron C, steel B, silver A, platinum S (see Weapons); two-proficiency weapons also need a grade one lower (at least E+) in a second type. Most companions start at D in their own weapon, so bronze is their first upgrade.
- After a battle the result comes in four beats, and a click, Space or Enter moves it on. First: won or lost, what it gave (gold, materials, XP) or cost the walls, and the company in a row of cards with their XP bars filling, each by their own share; a card shows the level, the XP they got, a skull on the face for every enemy they felled, gold marks beside the name of whoever earned the most contribution points (when one companion stands out), weapon and magic grades with what the battle added, anything learned, and INJURED across the face of anyone bound for the Infirmary (hover a card for the exact numbers). Then every level gained is shown on its own: the companion stands beside their ten stats and a light goes down the list one stat at a time; where it stops, the number goes up. The gains were already rolled when the XP was granted; the screen only reveals them in order. Then every combat art or spell learned is shown on its own, the companion beside it: its name, what it costs in large figures (stamina, or a spell's uses, and Recovery: how many of their next actions they sit out) and what it does, one thing to a line. An art that found a free place among the three carried is shown alone, with a line saying which place it took; so is a spell. When all three places were taken the art has been left behind and the panel becomes a comparison: the new art's card beside the cards of the three carried, each written out the same way (figures, then one line for each effect). Clicking a carried art leaves that one behind and carries the new one in its place; clicking it again, or the new art, puts things back. The comparison moves on only by its own "Go on", so a stray click cannot skip the choice. Last, the cards again and the way back to the city. The Drill Yard shows a level or an art gained from a drill the same way. During a battle a grade reached only raises a banner naming the grade.

### Magic

- Two schools: **Black Magic** (offence) and **White Magic** (support). Every companion has a grade in each, with the same grades and thresholds as weapons. Casting gives 4 points in the spell's school, up to 12 magic points per battle (separate from the weapon cap). Every school grade reached teaches a random spell of that school not yet known, with the same tier odds, no-duplicate rule and fall-through as combat arts, and like an art it is learned when the battle is over, not during it. All known spells can be cast; there are no slots. Each school has 12 spells, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/spells/`, all listed in `spell_library.tres`). A mage's signature spell is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other mage of that school can learn it.
- A spell costs one turn and no stamina, then a recovery of its tier + 1 (2-4; see Turns, Guard and the retreat for the cap). Each spell has a number of uses per companion (6 / 4 / 2 by tier, set per spell); uses last between battles and days and only Rest restores them.
- Black spells: damage = caster MAG + spell Mt - target RES (at least 1), with the normal accuracy and crit formulas using the spell's own Hit. They may strike every enemy, ignore part of RES, debuff the target (lowered stats, taking more damage, or damage every turn, which can kill: Blight), and delay the target's pending action: a pending action can be delayed only once; the next one it picks can be delayed again. Combat arts never touch countdowns.
- White spells always succeed: heals (caster MAG + the spell's power, on anyone still standing; an injured companion cannot be healed or reached by white magic at all), buffs, less damage taken, a veil (blows already aimed at the ally are drawn again among the others, and new aims fall on them less) or a beacon (every single blow already aimed moves to the ally, and new aims fall on them more), a reflecting ward (Thornward: like an art's stance, an enemy whose physical action lands on the ally is hurt for a share of the damage and can die of it; magic is never answered), and removing debuffs, on one ally or all.
- Enemy actions are physical (STR against DEF) or magic (MAG against RES); Guard halves both. The Goblin Wizard, the casters of the bestiary, the Rimeclaw Wyvern's frost, the Oathbreaker's Black Tide, and the Faceless Princess, Forsaken Seraph and Eclipse Sovereign are magic. Enemy MAG grows with the day like STR.
- **Magic weapons** are rare variants of the normal types: Attack and arts with them use MAG in place of STR in their type's formula (see Weapons), against RES, with the weapon's own Mt.
- **Effectiveness**: enemies carry traits (Flying and Large). Weapons, arts and spells list the traits they are effective against; every bow is effective against Flying and every spear against Large. An effective strike or spell deals half as much damage again: 50% of the damage left after DEF or RES, rounded down, added before a critical doubles it and before Guard or other damage-taken multipliers. Effective through both weapon and art still counts once. The share is `BattleConfig.EFFECTIVE_DAMAGE_BONUS_PERCENT`.

## Bosses

`data/enemies/bosses/` and `data/run/bosses/` (a boss group: the boss, then any escorts, who grow with the day; `description` is the one-line warning). Each boss day draws one from its pool by the run's seed (`Run.boss_draw`). Every boss has one idea:

- **Day 14**: The Horned Tyrant (its War Bellow stacks: it grows stronger the longer the fight runs), Mossback Troll (regenerates 18% of its HP on every Buff it shows: burst it down), Rampart Golem (high DEF, no RES, slow and crushing, braces to halve damage: magic, DEF-ignoring arts and Guard).
- **Day 28**: Rimeclaw Wyvern (flies, frost magic, Take Wing makes it very hard to hit: bows and accuracy), The Oathbreaker (its Oath of Thorns, a Defend, reflects 80% of weapon and art damage: read the intent, use spells), The Fallen Commander (with a One-Eye and a Flame Eagle: rallies every enemy and orders them to act sooner; alone it makes a last stand: choose the kill order).
- **Day 42**: The Faceless Princess (aims from the left and poisons: put the sturdiest on the left), The Forsaken Seraph (flies, magic, charges Judgement on everyone: RES, bows, speed and a delay spell), The Eclipse Sovereign (adds recovery to everyone; at half HP turns faster and harsher).

### Enemy mechanics

All in the battle rules and driven by data (`EnemyActionData`, `EnemyData`, `StatusEffectData`):
- **Damage over time** (`damage_per_tick`): at the end of every turn; Guard does not halve it. It can injure a companion, and ends with the injury. White magic that removes debuffs clears it.
- **Reflection** (`reflect_percent`): a weapon strike or combat art that lands on the bearer deals that share of its damage (at least 1) back to the striker. Spells are exempt.
- **Healing** (`heal_percent`) on the enemy itself, or on every enemy with the ALL_ALLIES targeting (which also spreads buffs).
- **The aim** (`EnemyUnit.pending_target`): an action on one companion draws its target when it is picked (`Battle.pick_next_action`, with `Battle.target_weights`: the place in the line, times whatever draws the eye or hides), and keeps it through delays and orders; the blow lands there. A new phase picks a new action, so a new aim. Once drawn it moves only when its target is injured (drawn again at once among those standing), when an effect that draws the eye lands on a companion (Beacon, the arts Challenge and Phalanx: every single blow already aimed moves to them), or when one that hides lands (Veil, the art Fade: blows aimed at them are drawn again among the others, and stay if nobody else stands). The weapon, traits and the place only count when an aim is drawn. Each move is an `aim` event (`enemy`, `target`, `from`, `why`: injured, drawn or hidden); the `intent` event carries the target. Guard does not draw the aim; it only halves the blow.
- **What is coming** (for the screen and the automated player): `Battle.incoming(c)` lists every pending action that will reach a companion (aimed at them, or on the whole line) in the order it lands, with its turns and its forecast as things stand now (0 damage for one that deals none); `Battle.falls_on_turn(c)` is the first turn on which those blows, all landing and none critical, with the damage over time already on them and a saving trait or Locket once, would fell them (0: they stand).
- **Criticals** (`EnemyActionData.crit_percent`): an enemy strikes critically only when its action says so, with that chance (no action does yet); its CRT is not used for it.
- **Reversed targeting** (`EnemyData.reversed_targeting` or per action): the position weights mirrored.
- **Orders** (`hasten_allies`): every other enemy's countdown drops by N, never below 1.
- **Recovery pressure** (`add_recovery`): adds to the recovery of each companion reached, on top of the cap. A lone companion still ignores recovery.
- **Phases** (`phase_trigger`: at an HP share, or when the last one standing): a second action list, a banner, an optional lasting effect, and the pending action is replaced.
- Also: stacking effects (`max_stacks`) and actions never picked twice in a row (`no_repeat`).

## Layout

- `scripts/battle/` battle rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, seeded `RandomNumberGenerator` injected. `battle_config.gd` holds every tuning number (grade thresholds, points per action, the per-battle cap, the tier odds per grade). `proficiency.gd` is the grade rules (including whether a companion may equip a weapon), `art_learning.gd` picks the art learned on a grade-up.
- `scripts/run/` the run rules: plain classes, no nodes or UI, every random choice from an injected seeded generator. `Run` (the day flow, city, gold and materials, roster and line, today's pending choice, the outcome; commands return false or a reason when not allowed), `RunCalendar` (the weekly pattern and where the events fall), `RunCompanion` (data, progress, HP between days), `Levels` (XP per level and the growth rolls), `RunInventory`/`OwnedWeapon` (the armory: each weapon held by at most one companion), `Front`, `BattleReport`, `Drill`/`DrillResult` (the training ground), `Gem` (the five stones), `Accessory` and `CraftOffer` (the Jeweler), and `run_config.gd` with every run tuning number (day counts, rewards, city damage, prices, enemy scaling). `CompanionProgress` is what a companion carries between battles: level, XP and stat gains, proficiency, known and equipped arts, and the weapon in hand; `Battle` reads and updates it.
- `scripts/data/` `Resource` classes for companions, enemies, weapons, combat arts, the art library, spells (`SpellData`) and the spell library, enemy traits (`EnemyTraits`), enemy actions and effects, companion traits (`TraitData`, its `TraitModifier`s, `TraitLibrary`; worked out by `scripts/battle/trait_rules.gd`), accessory types (`AccessoryTypeData`, `AccessoryLibrary`, and `AccessoryEffect`, which turns a type and a stone into a trait). `scripts/battle/spell_learning.gd` picks the spell learned on a school grade-up; `CombatMath.DamageType` is physical or magic.
- `data/` the `.tres` data: companions (stats, growth rates, weapon, starting grades, signature art), enemies (with their action lists; bosses in `enemies/bosses/`), weapons (`<tier>_<type>.tres`: wooden, bronze, iron, steel, silver and platinum of each of the five types, the 24 two-proficiency weapons `<tier>_<family>.tres`, and the fourteen rare weapons, the four magic ones among them), `arts/<weapon type>/` combat arts, `arts/art_library.tres` (every art that can be learned, signature arts included), `spells/black/` and `spells/white/` spells with `spells/spell_library.tres` (every spell that can be learned, the mages' signature spells included), `battles/prototype_battle.tres` (used by the battle tests), and `run/`: `run_content.tres` lists everything a run is built from: the companions, front `sites/`, enemy `groups/` (danger and the days they appear), `bosses/` (one per boss day), `events/`, `weapons` (the 30-weapon ladder the smithy, events and bosses draw from) `hybrid_weapons` (the 24 two-proficiency weapons) and `rare_weapons` (the fourteen rare weapons). A new event, group or rare weapon is a new `.tres` added to that list; no code.
- `scripts/ui/`, `scenes/main.tscn` (the main scene). `game.gd` shows the title (`title_screen.gd`) and then the city. `city_screen.gd` is the hub: it reads the run, sets each building's state, and opens the war table (`war_table.gd`), the barracks (`party_screen.gd`), the smithy and tavern (`store_screen.gd`), the scavenging table (`scavenge_screen.gd`), the Mason's Yard (`mason_screen.gd`), the Jeweler's room (`jeweler_screen.gd`), letters (`letter_panel.gd`: visits, events, the watch's report, the opening), the battle (`battle_screen.gd`) and the run-end screen over itself. The battle screen plays a battle the run prepared (`Run.start_battle`), emits `finished`, and shows the `BattleReport` the run hands back. Screens only read run and battle state and call their commands.
- The look: things from the world rather than an interface. `ui_theme.gd` holds the colours, the fonts (a book serif; system fonts for now) and the shared drawing (framed boards with cut corners and grain, parchment sheets). `SlantPanel`, `SlantBar`, `ActionButton` and `RowButton` are the shared pieces (the names date from when panels leaned; `UiTheme.SLANT` is 0 now); the two buttons have a `paper` style for lines written on parchment. In battle, `action_menu.gd` is the close-up and the orders (light on a soft-edged veil of shadow, so the art and the field show through; `ActionButton`'s `veil` style) and `party_panel.gd` one portrait of the line; in the Barracks, `muster_row.gd` is one of the company in the list, which can be carried and set down. Companion art (`assets/character/`) is a cut-out with no background: wherever a face is shown in a frame or a ring the UI paints a plain ground behind it, and `CompanionData.figure_scale` evens out how large each was painted so heads match side by side (`portrait_focus` says where the face is). `ui_icons.gd` draws the small pictures. `art_facts.gd` sets out an art's or a spell's cost and effect to be read at a glance (the learned panel `learned_panel.gd`, the battle orders and the barracks sheet use it). `stat_table.gd` writes a companion's stats and, apart from them, their growth rates (the barracks sheet and a hire's paper use it); `store_screen.gd` the tavern and the smithy, and `training_screen.gd` the drill yard. After a battle, `battle_result.gd` runs the result (`spoils_card.gd` is one companion's card, `level_up_panel.gd` the stat-by-stat reveal of one level).
- The city is drawn in code, with no image files: `city_art.gd` is the kit (walls, roofs, windows, doors, flames), `city_building.gd` the ten buildings, `city_backdrop.gd` the scene and its times of day (`set_phase`), `interior_backdrop.gd` the inside of a building, `stage_backdrop.gd` the battlefield. Where each building stands is the `RECTS` table in `city_building.gd`. Painted art can replace any of these later without touching the screens.
- The layout is designed at 1920x1080 and scales with the window.
- `tests/` headless tests: `run_tests.gd` (battle rules, and the runner), `run_rule_tests.gd` (run rules and the playthrough), `run_policy.gd` (the automated player), `magic_tests.gd`, `bestiary_tests.gd`, `weapon_tests.gd`, `training_tests.gd`, `infirmary_tests.gd`, `rare_weapon_tests.gd`, `scavenge_tests.gd`, `trait_tests.gd`, `trait_hook_tests.gd` (the traits of single moments, and later waves), `city_tests.gd` (the city's upgrades, materials and enchanting), `accessory_tests.gd` (the Jeweler, crafting and the accessories).
- `data/traits/<family>/` one `.tres` per companion trait and `data/traits/trait_library.tres` listing those handed out; written by `tools/generate_traits.gd` (`godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/generate_traits.gd`); a trait can also be written by hand as a `.tres` and added to the library.
- `data/accessories/<family>/` one `.tres` per accessory type and `data/accessories/accessory_library.tres`; written by `tools/generate_accessories.gd` (run the same way), whose table holds every type's five numbers.
- `tools/screen_checks/` scripts that open the real game, click through one screen and save screenshots, for checking UI work by eye (see its `README.md`). They are not tests and nothing runs them automatically.
- `tools/battle_probe.gd` measures how battles play out (run like the generators; `PROBE_RUNS`, `PROBE_REPLAYS`): the automated player plays whole runs, and every battle it meets is replayed from the same start by four players (the automated one, one that lets the strongest act, one that attacks at random, and one that reads the enemy's aims: it fells whoever is about to bring a companion down, or has that companion guard). It prints how long battles are, how the actions are shared along the line, how hard enemy blows are, how often the same battle is won again, and how the four compare. Not a test. `design/battle.md` is written from its figures; its last section keeps the figures after each step.
- `assets/` character and enemy art.

## Weapons

Six tiers, and every one of the five types (sword, axe, spear, bow, dagger) exists in every tier: 30 weapons (`WeaponData.tier`, `RunContent.weapons`). Mt = the tier's base, 2 a tier (wooden 0, bronze 2, iron 4, steel 6, silver 8, platinum 10) + the type's offset (sword 0, spear +1, bow -1, dagger -1; the axe 0 from wooden to iron, +1 at steel and silver, +2 at platinum), never below 0 (`BattleConfig.WEAPON_TIER_BASE_MIGHT`, `WEAPON_TYPE_MIGHT_OFFSET`, `WEAPON_TIER_GRADE`).

| Tier (grade) | Sword | Axe | Spear | Bow | Dagger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wooden (E) | 0 / 90 / 4 | 0 / 80 / 7 | 1 / 85 / 6 | 0 / 90 / 3 | 0 / 95 / 1 |
| Bronze (D) | 2 / 90 / 5 | 2 / 80 / 9 | 3 / 85 / 7 | 1 / 90 / 4 | 1 / 95 / 2 |
| Iron (C) | 4 / 90 / 6 | 4 / 80 / 10 | 5 / 85 / 8 | 3 / 90 / 5 | 3 / 95 / 2 |
| Steel (B) | 6 / 90 / 7 | 7 / 80 / 11 | 7 / 85 / 9 | 5 / 90 / 6 | 5 / 95 / 3 |
| Silver (A) | 8 / 90 / 7 | 9 / 85 / 11 | 9 / 85 / 9 | 7 / 90 / 6 | 7 / 95 / 3 |
| Platinum (S) | 10 / 95 / 7 | 12 / 85 / 11 | 11 / 90 / 9 | 9 / 90 / 6 | 9 / 100 / 3 |

Mt / Hit / weight. Axes are the least accurate and heaviest, daggers the lightest and most accurate, bows as accurate as swords (90 at every tier); steel is no less accurate than iron or silver and no heavier than silver; wooden is the lightest tier. Every bow is effective against Flying, every spear against Large.

- **Damage**: attack power + Mt (+ an art's bonus) - the target's DEF, at least 1. Attack power depends on the weapon type: sword and spear STR; axe STR + BLD / 2; bow (STR + DEX) / 2; dagger (STR + SPD) / 2; every half rounded down, from the stats as they stand in battle (after the weight penalty and any effects). A magic weapon uses MAG in place of STR (a magic bow is (MAG + DEX) / 2) and strikes against RES. Enemies (STR or MAG + their action's Mt) and spells (MAG + the spell's Mt) are not affected by weapon type. The rule is `CombatMath.attack_power`; between battles `RunCompanion.attack_power(w)` gives it from the stats a companion carries, and `RunCompanion.attack(w)` adds the weapon's Mt (the "Attack" figure: what a strike deals before DEF).
- **The magic weapons** are four of the rare weapons (see Rare weapons): Emberglass Sword Mt 3 and Whisper Fang (dagger) Mt 2 need E+; Moonthread Bow Mt 3 and Stormcall Pike Mt 4 need D. Each is above the bronze weapon of its type in Mt and at most the iron one and strikes with MAG against RES; 150 gold at the Smithy. The hermit sells the Emberglass Sword.
- **The Smithy** rolls its stock on the first day of each week: 5 different weapons, each with a tier drawn by the week (`RunConfig.SHOP_TIER_WEIGHTS`): week 1 wooden and bronze; week 2 adds iron; week 3 steel; week 4 a little silver; weeks 5-6 mostly steel, some silver. Each slot is a two-proficiency weapon of that tier with a 20% chance when the tier has them (bronze to silver; `RunConfig.SHOP_HYBRID_CHANCE`), otherwise a ladder weapon of a random type (a slot that rolls a weapon already in stock is drawn again, so about 23% of bronze-silver slots end up two-proficiency). It never sells platinum. Prices by tier: 20, 50, 110, 200, 320, 480 gold; two-proficiency weapons 65, 140, 250, 400 (`Run.weapon_price`). On top of the five, each week has a 35% chance of one rare weapon (see Rare weapons), listed first; then by tier and type, a two-proficiency weapon after the ladder weapon of its type.
- **Events** that grant a weapon either give a set one or draw one when the event comes up: a tier drawn as the week's stock is, or (one time in four) one tier above the best the Smithy sells that week; then a two-proficiency weapon of that tier (and type) with the same 20% chance; and with a 15% chance a rare weapon of that type whose grade fits the week instead (`RunConfig.EVENT_RARE_CHANCE`). A Wounded Knight leaves a bow; the Ruined Armory any type.
- **Bosses**: beating the day 14 or day 28 boss drops one random weapon (any type) a tier above the best the Smithy sells that week: steel on day 14, platinum on day 28 (`Run.boss_weapon_tier`); on day 14 a two-proficiency steel weapon with the same 20% chance (there is no platinum one). With a 50% chance (`RunConfig.BOSS_RARE_CHANCE`) the boss drops a rare weapon in its place: on day 14 one needing grade D or less, on day 28 any. It goes to the armory and the battle's report names it (`BattleReport.weapon_found`). The day 42 boss ends the run and drops nothing.

### Rare weapons

Off the metal ladder: a rare weapon needs only a grade in its type (not a tier) and has one property no ladder weapon has. There are fourteen (`RunContent.rare_weapons`; `WeaponData.rare`, and `WeaponData.trait_lines()` writes the property for display): the four magic weapons above and these ten.

| Weapon | Type, grade | Mt / Hit / weight | Property | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oathkeeper | sword, D | grows / 90 / 5 | Mt is the ladder base Mt of the tier the wielder's sword grade can hold: 2 at D, 4 at C, 6 at B, 8 at A, 10 at S | 300 |
| Twinfang Sabre | sword, C | 2 / 80 / 6 | a plain Attack strikes twice, each rolled on its own (arts keep their own count) | 280 |
| Headsman | axe, C | 7 / 60 / 13 | Crit +25, Attack and arts | 280 |
| Bloodletter | axe, D | 3 / 70 / 9 | heals the wielder for half the damage it deals (rounded down, never above max HP) | 180 |
| Wallbreaker | spear, C | 3 / 75 / 10 | ignores half the target's DEF; with an art that ignores DEF too, the larger counts | 280 |
| Sentinel's Pike | spear, D | 3 / 80 / 9 | DEF +3 while held, and enemies are 1.5x as likely to aim at the wielder | 180 |
| Galewing Bow | bow, D | 2 / 90 / 3 | SPD +3 while held | 180 |
| Longwatch | bow, C | 5 / 110 / 7 | Crit +15 | 280 |
| Viper's Kiss | dagger, D | 1 / 95 / 2 | every hit that lands poisons: 2 damage a turn for 3 turns (a kill by it is the wielder's) | 180 |
| Magpie | dagger, C | 3 / 90 / 3 | +10 gold for every enemy its wielder fells in a won battle, in the battle's gold | 280 |

Spears are effective against Large and bows against Flying, as every spear and bow is. The properties are data on `WeaponData` (`might_by_grade`, `attack_strikes`, `crit_bonus`, `lifesteal_percent`, `ignore_def_percent`, `held_stats`, `target_weight_mult`, `on_hit_effect`, `gold_per_kill`), so another rare weapon is a new `.tres` in that list. The Attack figure and every forecast show the true numbers (Oathkeeper's Mt for its holder, two strikes for Twinfang).

Where they come from: each weekly Smithy restock has a 35% chance (`RunConfig.SHOP_RARE_CHANCE`) of one rare weapon, drawn from those whose grade fits the week (`RunConfig.RARE_GRADE_BY_WEEK`: none in week 1, grade D or less in week 2, any from week 3); the day 14 and day 28 bosses (50%) and events that give a weapon (15%), as under Weapons.

On screen a rare weapon carries a small red diamond before its name and the word "rare" before its type, in the Smithy's list and in the Barracks' armory, and each of its properties is written on a line of its own under the usual small print. In the Barracks Mt is what it would be in the chosen companion's hand (Oathkeeper's follows their grade; the Smithy writes "Mt by grade"). In battle the forecast of a plain Attack with a weapon that strikes twice reads "DMG 9x2" as an art's does, the sheet shown while Shift is held lists the held weapon's properties and, lower down, each of the companion's traits with what it does, and pointing at the weapon's line under the companion's name does too. After a won battle the card of whoever holds Magpie says in its hover text what the weapon brought in.

### Two-proficiency weapons

Six families need a grade in two types. Each **is its main type** (the first named) for everything: the attack power formula, the proficiency gained by using it, which arts can be used with it, effectiveness. The second type is only a requirement: using the weapon never raises it, so it rises only by training (see Training ground; a drill can open a weapon type or school from E) or by fighting with a weapon of that type, or comes from the companion's starting grades.

| Family | Main + second |
|---|---|
| Halberd | axe + spear |
| Glaive | spear + sword (effective against Large) |
| Greatsword | sword + axe |
| Twinblades | dagger + sword |
| Javelin | spear + bow (effective against Large) |
| Runeblade | sword + black magic: a magic weapon (MAG in place of STR, against RES) |

Each family exists at bronze, iron, steel and silver ("Bronze Halberd" ... "Silver Halberd"; `data/weapons/<tier>_<family>.tres`, listed in `RunContent.hybrid_weapons`, 24 in all). To hold one: the tier's grade in the main type and one grade lower in the second, but never below E+ (everyone has E in everything, so E would be no requirement; `BattleConfig.HYBRID_SECOND_GRADE_MIN`): bronze D + E+, iron C + D, steel B + C, silver A + B. Mt is that of the main type's ladder weapon one tier up (`BattleConfig.HYBRID_MIGHT_TIERS_UP`), weight 2 above the main type's weapon of the same tier, 4 for the Twinblades (`HYBRID_WEIGHT_BONUS`, `HYBRID_WEIGHT_BONUS_BY_FAMILY`), Hit as it; the price is the tier's x1.25, rounded to 5 (`RunConfig.HYBRID_PRICE_MULT`). Effective only per the main type: Glaive and Javelin against Large, the others against nothing.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Iron (C + D) | 7 / 80 / 12 | 7 / 85 / 10 | 6 / 90 / 8 | 5 / 95 / 6 | 7 / 85 / 10 | 6 / 90 / 8 | 140 |
| Steel (B + C) | 9 / 80 / 13 | 9 / 85 / 11 | 8 / 90 / 9 | 7 / 95 / 7 | 9 / 85 / 11 | 8 / 90 / 9 | 250 |
| Silver (A + B) | 12 / 85 / 13 | 11 / 85 / 11 | 10 / 90 / 9 | 9 / 95 / 7 | 11 / 85 / 11 | 10 / 90 / 9 | 400 |

Mt / Hit / weight. `WeaponData.second_type` (a weapon type) or `second_school` (a magic school), with `second_grade`, hold the second requirement (-1 / unset for ordinary weapons); `WeaponData.requirement_text()` writes it for display ("axe C · spear D"; an ordinary weapon "spear C"). Every check of whether someone can hold a weapon goes through `CompanionProgress.weapon_block_reason(w)` (`can_equip_weapon` is it being empty), which names the first grade missing ("Needs spear D; Elena is E.").

## Combat arts

An art belongs to one weapon type, can only be used with a weapon of that type in hand, and costs stamina and a recovery like any action (always one turn). Tier 1: 4-6 STA, sits out 2 actions; tier 2: 8-10 STA, 3 actions; tier 3: 13-15 STA, 4 actions (never more than there are others standing). Dagger arts cost what the other types' do; Twin Arrows strikes twice at Mt -1. Every weapon type has 12 arts, 5 / 4 / 3 by tier (`data/arts/`, all listed in `art_library.tres`). A companion's signature art is one of the twelve: they start knowing it, and the other user of that weapon can learn it. Effects are limited to bonus Mt/Hit/Crit, striking every enemy, striking twice, ignoring part of DEF, effectiveness against a trait, a debuff on each enemy hit (lowered stats, some of which stack; taking more damage; bleeding or poison every turn, which can kill) and a buff on the user (raised stats; taking less damage; being aimed at more or less often; a stance that answers physical blows: an enemy whose physical action lands on the user is hurt for a share of the damage, at least 1, and can die of it; magic is never answered). `WeaponData.unique_art` is reserved for rare weapons that grant an art of their own; nothing uses it yet, nor `CombatArtData.durability_cost`.
